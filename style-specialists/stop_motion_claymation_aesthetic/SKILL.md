# SKILL: Stop-Motion & Claymation Aesthetic
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Frame-by-Frame Animation Language

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of stop-motion animation and claymation aesthetics for AI-generated campaigns. This skill encodes the visual grammar of frame-by-frame animation: the tactile texture of physical materials, the slight jitter of hand-manipulated movement, the thumbprint evidence of human touch, and the specific charm of imperfection that distinguishes stop-motion from fluid CGI. Essential for whimsical brand stories, holiday campaigns, product demonstrations, and any content requiring tangible warmth and handmade authenticity.

---

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**The Stop-Motion Illusion:**
Stop-motion creates movement by photographing physical objects in tiny increments (typically 12–24 frames per second). The resulting motion has distinct characteristics:
- **Jitter:** Micro-irregularities between frames create nervous, alive energy
- **Texture:** Physical materials — clay, wool, paper, metal — retain real-world surface detail
- **Light Flicker:** Slight exposure variations between frames add organic warmth
- **Gravity:** Physical objects obey real physics; no impossible CG weightlessness
- **Fingerprints:** Evidence of human manipulation visible on malleable surfaces

**Frame Rate & Motion Quality:**

| FPS | Motion Character | Emotional Effect | Best For |
|-----|-----------------|------------------|----------|
| **12 fps** | Visible stepping, dreamy, handmade | Nostalgia, craft, fairy tale | Claymation, indie, artistic |
| **15 fps** | Slightly smoother but still tactile | Whimsical, charming, accessible | TV commercials, explainers |
| **24 fps** | Cinema standard, smoother but retains texture | Professional, polished, cinematic | Feature films (Laika, Aardman) |
| **Ones vs. Twos** | Every frame unique (ones) vs. each pose held 2 frames (twos) | Ones = nervous energy; Twos = weight and deliberation | Twos standard; ones for fast action |

**Material Categories:**

| Material | Visual Signature | Movement Character | Prompt Terms |
|----------|-----------------|-------------------|--------------|
| **Plasticine Clay** | Fingerprints, soft edges, color blending | Malleable, squash-and-stretch, impressionable | "plasticine clay", "fingerprints visible", "soft malleable form" |
| **Foam Latex** | Skin-like, pore detail, painted surface | Flexible but structured, puppet-like | "foam latex puppet", "painted skin texture" |
| **Wool/Felt** | Fibrous surface, fuzzy edges, textile quality | Soft movement, slight vibration | "felted wool", "fibrous texture", "soft textile surface" |
| **Paper/Cut-Out** | Sharp edges, layered depth, graphic quality | Flat movement, hinge joints, 2.5D | "paper cut-out", "layered construction", "graphic flatness" |
| **Wood/Puppet** | Grain visible, carved detail, jointed limbs | Mechanical, hinged, traditional | "wooden puppet", "carved detail", "ball-jointed" |
| **Metal/Armature** | Reflective, machined, industrial | Rigid, precise, heavy | "metal armature", "machined joints", "industrial puppet" |

**The Armature Reality:**
Internal metal skeletons allow puppets to hold poses but create physical constraints:
- **Joint Limits:** Puppets cannot move beyond mechanical joint range
- **Weight Distribution:** Heavy heads require support; movement shows effort
- **Foot Attachment:** Tie-down screws visible in feet or magnetic bases used
- **Scale:** Typically 1:6 to 1:12 scale for manageability
- **Surface Imperfection:** Paint chips, dust, fingerprints accumulate across shoot

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Claymation Character**
- Material: Colored plasticine, fingerprints visible, soft edges
- Features: Slightly asymmetrical, thumb-pressed eyes, hand-sculpted mouth
- Movement: Squash-and-stretch exaggeration, slight wobble when standing
- Surface: Dust and lint visible; color slightly blended at joints from manipulation
- Emotional: Childlike, innocent, vulnerable, handmade with love
- Best For: Children's content, educational, heartfelt brand stories, holidays

**2. The Laika-Style Puppet**
- Material: Foam latex skin over metal armature, painted detail
- Features: Highly detailed, realistic proportions, expressive face replacement
- Movement: 24fps, subtle, nuanced, approaches realism while retaining tactile quality
- Surface: Skin pores visible, fabric clothing with real weave, hair individually placed
- Emotional: Dark fairy tale, sophisticated, slightly uncanny, emotionally complex
- Best For: Premium brand stories, dark whimsy, adult animation, feature quality

**3. The Paper Cut-Out World**
- Material: Colored paper, card stock, layered depth
- Features: Flat graphic quality, sharp edges, hinge joints at limbs
- Movement: 2.5D — layers slide past each other creating parallax
- Surface: Paper grain visible, slight curl at edges, shadow between layers
- Emotional: Graphic, direct, storybook, accessible, modern illustration
- Best For: Explainers, brand origin stories, social campaigns, modern playful

**4. The Miniature Set**
- Scale: 1:12 or 1:24 tabletop environments
- Construction: Real materials at small scale — wood, fabric, metal, plants
- Detail: Every element physically built; no digital extension
- Lighting: Practical sources visible; matches scale (tiny lamps, LED strips)
- Depth: Real depth of field from macro lenses; backgrounds genuinely out of focus
- Emotional: Nostalgia, dollhouse intimacy, wish-fulfillment, craft obsession
- Best For: Product showcases, holiday villages, architectural models, food sets

**5. The Replacement Animation**
- Technique: Slightly different sculpt/pose for each frame (e.g., facial expressions)
- Result: Impossible smoothness within stop-motion; morphing quality
- Surface: Subtle changes between frames create living, breathing texture
- Emotional: Dreamlike, fluid within handmade context, magical realism
- Best For: Transformations, emotional shifts, surreal sequences, peak moments

### STOP-MOTION PRODUCTION DETAILS

**Visible Evidence of Process:**
- **Tie-Down Holes:** Tiny screws or holes in feet for stability (sometimes visible)
- **Fingerprints:** Especially on clay; can be smoothed or left as character
- **Dust & Lint:** Accumulates during long shoots; becomes part of texture
- **Paint Wear:** Chips and rubs at joints from repeated manipulation
- **Light Shifts:** Slight warm/cool shifts between shooting days
- **Set Bumps:** Micro-movements of background elements between frames

**Camera Characteristics:**
- **Macro Lenses:** Extreme close-up capability; shallow depth of field
- **Aperture:** Often f/5.6–f/11 for sufficient depth on small subjects
- **Focus Pulling:** Manual rack focus between miniature planes
- **Motion Control:** Computer-controlled camera moves for smooth dolly/shots
- **Frame Grabs:** Each frame is a high-resolution still photograph

### TECHNICAL NOTES FOR AI GENERATION
- Use "stop-motion", "claymation", or "frame-by-frame" as primary style signals
- Specify material explicitly: "plasticine clay", "felted wool", "paper cut-out"
- Mention fingerprints or thumbprints for clay; mention fiber texture for wool
- For FLUX: describe tactile surface details in positive terms
- For LTX: specify "subtle frame-by-frame position shifts" rather than smooth motion
- Scale is important — mention "miniature", "tabletop", or "small-scale"
- Lighting should feel physical and practical rather than infinite softbox
- Imperfections sell the effect: "slight dust", "paint chips", "surface lint"
- Reference studios for style anchors: "Aardman", "Laika", "Rankin/Bass", "Tim Burton"
