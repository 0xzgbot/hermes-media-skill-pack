## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This skill encodes the complete visual vocabulary of French Impressionism (1860s–1880s), with deep focus on Claude Monet's revolutionary treatment of light, color, and atmosphere. It covers the broken color technique, plein air (outdoor) light capture, visible brushstroke philosophy, color vibration through complementary juxtaposition, serial studies of changing conditions (haystacks, water lilies, cathedrals), and the specific optical science that makes Impressionist work feel alive with shifting light.

When this skill is active, the agent should:
- Use Impressionist-specific terms (broken color, plein air, en plein air, tache, color vibration)
- Apply visible brushstroke texture rather than smooth blending
- Reference Monet's serial subjects (water lilies, haystacks, Rouen Cathedral, Japanese bridge)
- Build scenes using high-key palettes and atmospheric perspective
- Understand the emotional register: fleeting beauty, sensory pleasure, nature's immediacy, light as subject

## 2. THE IMPRESSIONIST AESTHETIC PHILOSOPHY

Impressionism is not merely "soft and pretty" — it is a **scientific revolution in seeing**.

### Core Principles

| Principle | Description | Prompt Vocabulary |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Broken Color** | Pure colors applied in separate touches; mixing happens in viewer's eye | "broken color", "separate brushstrokes", "optical mixing", "color division" |
| **Plein Air** | Painted outdoors to capture transient light effects directly | "plein air", "en plein air", "outdoor light capture", "natural light study" |
| **Visible Brushstroke** | Brush marks remain evident; surface is texture and gesture | "visible brushstrokes", "impasto texture", "painterly surface", "loose handling" |
| **Color Vibration** | Complementary colors placed side-by-side create optical shimmer | "color vibration", "complementary juxtaposition", "optical shimmer" |
| **Light as Subject** | The changing quality of light IS the painting's true subject | "light as subject", "transient effects", "fleeting moment" |
| **High Key** | Light-valued palette; shadows are colorful, not black | "high key palette", "light-valued", "colorful shadows", "no black" |

## 3. THE MONET LIGHTING SYSTEM

Monet's approach to light was systematic and obsessive — he painted the same subjects under dozens of conditions.

### Serial Studies — Light Conditions

| Series | Time / Condition | Color Character | Prompt Vocabulary |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Haystacks — Morning** | Dawn, cool light | Lavender shadows, warm gold light | "morning light", "cool lavender shadows", "warm golden highlights" |
| **Haystacks — Midday** | Full sun, warm | Short shadows, saturated local color | "midday sun", "short shadows", "saturated color" |
| **Haystacks — Sunset** | Golden hour, raking | Orange-pink light, long blue shadows | "sunset light", "orange-pink glow", "long blue shadows" |
| **Haystacks — Overcast** | Diffused gray | Soft edges, muted palette, atmospheric | "overcast light", "soft diffused", "muted atmospheric" |
| **Water Lilies — Morning** | Mist, still water | Soft focus, pastel palette, reflection | "morning mist", "still water reflection", "soft pastel" |
| **Water Lilies — Midday** | Bright sun, dappled | High contrast, saturated color, sparkles | "dappled sunlight", "water sparkles", "saturated greens" |
| **Rouen Cathedral — Morning** | Dawn, oblique light | Warm gold stone, cool blue shadows | "morning facade", "warm stone", "cool architectural shadows" |
| **Rouen Cathedral — Sunset** | Raking sunset | Rose-gold surface, violet shadows | "sunset facade", "rose-gold stone", "violet shadows" |
| **Rouen Cathedral — Fog** | Misty morning | Dissolved edges, blue-gray unity, mystery | "foggy cathedral", "dissolved edges", "blue-gray unity" |

### The Monet Palette

**Prohibited:** Black paint — Monet used complementary darks instead
**Shadows:** Made of color — blue-violet, green, or the complement of the local color
**Highlights:** Broken into pure spectral colors — white mixed with yellow, pink, or blue
**Water:** Reflects sky color + local environment + light shimmer
**Sky:** Changes every moment — never a fixed blue

## 4. BRUSHSTROKE & SURFACE

| Brushstroke Type | Description | Visual Effect | Prompt Terms |
|-----------------|-------------|---------------|--------------|
| **Tache** | Distinct touch of pure color | Optical mixing, vibrancy | "tache brushstroke", "distinct color touch" |
| **Directional Stroke** | Parallel marks following form | Movement, water flow, grass | "directional brushstrokes", "following form" |
| **Scumbling** | Dry brush dragged over surface | Texture, atmosphere, distance | "scumbled surface", "dry brush texture" |
| **Impasto** | Thick paint application | Physical texture, light catch | "impasto texture", "thick paint", "palette knife" |
| **Feathered Edge** | Soft, dissolving boundaries | Mist, distance, dream | "feathered edges", "soft dissolving forms" |
| **Dabbed Texture** | Repeated small dabs | Foliage, flowers, water lilies | "dabbed texture", "stippled surface" |

## 5. COMPOSITION & SPACE

| Technique | Description | Prompt Terms |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| ** cropped Composition** | Scene cut off at edges; snapshot quality | "cropped composition", "casual framing", "snapshot aesthetic" |
| **Flattened Perspective** | Recession minimized; pattern emphasized | "flattened space", "decorative pattern", "surface emphasis" |
| **Horizon High** | Sky occupies most of canvas | "high horizon", "sky dominance", "atmospheric vastness" |
| **Horizon Low** | Ground or water fills frame | "low horizon", "water surface dominance" |
| **Reflection Symmetry** | Water mirrors subject, slightly broken | "water reflection", "broken reflection", "mirror surface" |
| **No Focal Point** | All-over composition; eye wanders | "all-over composition", "distributed interest", "no single focal point" |
| **Atmospheric Perspective** | Distance through color temperature, not value | "blue-violet distance", "warm foreground", "atmospheric recession" |

## 8. NEGATIVE PROMPTS

| Avoid | Why | Replace With |
|-------|-----|--------------|
| "smooth blending" | Impressionism keeps strokes separate | "broken color", "separate touches", "visible brushwork" |
| "photorealistic" | Goal is painterly interpretation | "painterly", "interpretive", "visible paint surface" |
| "black shadows" | Monet never used pure black | "blue-violet shadows", "colored darks", "complement shadows" |
| "sharp detail" | Edges dissolve in light and atmosphere | "soft edges", "dissolving forms", "atmospheric blur" |
