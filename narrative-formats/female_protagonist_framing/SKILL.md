# SKILL: Female Protagonist Framing
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Character & Direction System

---

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of cinematographic framing that signals female agency, interiority, and autonomous perspective. Moves beyond objectifying or passive framing to compositions that emphasize decision-making capacity, spatial ownership, and subjective gaze. Draws from feminist film theory and contemporary cinematography practices that center female protagonists as active narrative drivers rather than decorative elements [^39^].

### TRIGGER KEYWORDS
female protagonist, woman hero, female lead, woman's perspective, female gaze, female agency, woman protagonist, leading woman, female character, heroine, strong female, woman in action, female-centered, feminine perspective

### CORE RULES
- Frame women as subjects with gaze, not objects of gaze — camera looks with her, not at her
- Spatial ownership: protagonist occupies space confidently, not tentatively
- Eye-level framing signals equality; high angle reduces power unless narratively motivated
- Action and decision frames: show her making choices, not reacting to others' choices
- Avoid fragmentation: no isolated body parts unless character shares this POV
- Interiority signals: close-ups of face during decision moments reveal subjective experience
- Her gaze drives the scene: what she looks at matters — camera follows her attention

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**Agency Signals — Composition:**
- Centered Power: Subject placed in geometric center or dominant thirds intersection
- Eye-Line Height: Camera at subject eye level (not looking down or up unnecessarily)
- Forward Motion: Subject moving toward camera or into frame space (not retreating)
- Spatial Ownership: Subject occupies majority of frame real estate; environment frames her rather than consumes her
- Leading Room: Space in front of gaze/direction of movement (not behind)
- Hands Visible: Hands in active positions (gesturing, holding tools, touching environment) — signals capability and action

**Power Composition:**
- Low Angle Restraint: Low angles signal power but overuse becomes heroic cliché; use selectively for decisive moments
- High Angle Avoidance: Avoid looking down on female subject unless she is literally in a pit/underground — high angle historically signals weakness
- Symmetry: Central symmetry = authority and control; asymmetry = dynamism and choice
- Vertical Dominance: Subject's head in upper frame third with strong posture; not cut off at neck or knees
- Environmental Scale: Subject proportional to environment (not dwarfed unless narratively justified)

**Gaze Direction & The Female Gaze:**
- Direct Camera Gaze: Subject looks into lens = confrontation, confidence, breaking fourth wall
- Off-Camera Gaze: Subject looks at off-screen object = interior thought, observation, agency of attention
- Reflected Gaze: Subject's reflection in mirror/window/glass showing what she sees and that she is seeing [^39^]
- Mutual Gaze: Two-shot where female subject's eye-line is higher or equal to counterpart's
- Downward Gaze: Subject looks down at object/tool/map = intellectual engagement, not submission
- Upward Gaze: Subject looks up at sky/building = aspiration, scale, ambition

**Lens & Framing Choices:**
- Focal Length: 35–50mm for natural perspective; 85mm for intimate power; avoid ultra-wide distortion on face
- Close-Up: Tight on face with context visible (not floating head); environment matters to her story
- Medium Shot: Waist-up with hands visible; most versatile for dialogue + gesture
- Wide Shot: Subject small but central in vast environment = isolation, journey, self-determination

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Decisive Moment Frame**
- Composition: Subject in center, body angled toward action
- Hands: On a door handle, weapon, steering wheel, tool, phone
- Gaze: Off-camera at objective; determined, not pleading
- Emotional: Agency, decision, forward momentum
- Best For: Thriller, action, drama turning points

**2. Interiority Close-Up**
- Framing: Tight close-up but with environment reflected in eyes or visible in background
- Gaze: Off-camera, contemplative, not directed at person
- Light: Soft side-light revealing thought; window light for introspection
- Emotional: Complex inner life, intelligence, observation
- Best For: Drama, psychological depth, character study [^39^]

**3. Reflected Gaze (Mirror/Window)**
- Setup: Subject looks at reflection; audience sees both subject and what she sees
- Technique: Reflection in train window, mirror, glass door, water surface
- Effect: Emphasizes that she is the one looking; her perception is central [^39^]
- Emotional: Self-awareness, duality, observation as power
- Best For: Opening scenes, turning points, self-realization

**4. Walking Into Power**
- Movement: Subject walks toward camera or through frame from background to foreground
- Camera: Static or slow tracking; she controls the movement
- Composition: Leading room in front; she claims space
- Emotional: Confidence, arrival, taking control
- Best For: Introductions, power shifts, climactic entrances

**5. The Active Hand Frame**
- Focus: Hands doing something (typing, building, fighting, creating)
- Framing: Medium shot or close-up with hands in lower frame
- Gaze: Often down at hands or forward at result
- Emotional: Competence, skill, creation, capability
- Best For: Professional contexts, action, craft, science

**6. Equal Gaze Two-Shot**
- Setup: Female subject and counterpart in same frame
- Height: Eye levels equal or female subject slightly higher
- Positioning: Female subject on left (traditionally power side in Western composition)
- Emotional: Partnership, equality, mutual respect
- Best For: Romance, negotiation, partnership scenes

### AGENCY COMPOSITION MATRIX
| Framing Element | Agency Signal | Weakness Signal | Correction |
|-----------------|---------------|-----------------|------------|
| Camera Height | Eye level | High angle looking down | Lower camera to subject's eye line |
| Subject Position | Center/dominant third | Edge of frame, background | Move to power position |
| Gaze Direction | Off-camera at objective | Looking up pleadingly | Level gaze or looking down at task |
| Body Language | Forward lean, hands active | Retreating, hands hidden | Open posture, visible hands |
| Leading Room | Space in front of movement | Space behind (retreating) | Compose with forward space |
| Environment Scale | Proportional to subject | Subject dwarfed without reason | Balance subject and setting |
