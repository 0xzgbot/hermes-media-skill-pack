# SKILL: 60-Second Brand Film
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Genre & Format Expertise

---

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of the 60-second brand film — the premium storytelling format that allows genuine three-act micro-structure. With twice the breathing room of a TV spot, brand films can establish character, develop emotional stakes, and deliver satisfying payoff while maintaining brand integration throughout. Research shows attention drops after 30 seconds, so the second half must escalate or pay off the first [^35^][^47^].

---

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**Three-Act Micro-Structure:**
- Act I — Setup (0–15s): Establish world, character, and emotional stakes
- Act II — Confrontation/Turning Point (15–40s): Conflict, discovery, or emotional escalation
- Act III — Resolution/Payoff (40–60s): Transformation, brand reveal, emotional catharsis [^35^]
- Beat Density: ~5 plot beats across 60 seconds; one beat every 12 seconds
- Reversal Requirement: At least one narrative reversal or surprise in Act II [^42^]

**Emotional Payoff Engineering:**
- Setup Promise: First 15 seconds must promise an emotional destination
- Midpoint Shift: Second act must complicate or deepen the promise (not just repeat)
- Payoff Delivery: Final 15 seconds must fulfill the promise with brand as enabler
- Catharsis Timing: Emotional peak at 45–50 seconds; brand resolution at 50–60 seconds
- Attention Management: Viewer attention averages 19% of 60-second duration — front-load best material [^47^]

**Brand Integration Strategy:**
- Native Integration: Brand appears as natural part of story world (not tacked-on)
- Early Seed: Brand element visible in Act I (color, shape, philosophy) even before reveal
- Act II Deepening: Product/service solves escalating problem or enables emotional moment
- Act III Revelation: Brand name/logo appears as organic conclusion, not interruption
- Philosophy Over Product: Brand values and worldview prioritized over feature list

**Pacing Architecture:**
- Act I: Slower, atmospheric, world-building shots (3–5 seconds per cut)
- Act II: Accelerating pace, rising tension (2–3 seconds per cut)
- Act III: Climax cut rate peaks, then resolves to slower final shot
- Rhythm: Like breathing — inhale (Act I), hold/intensify (Act II), exhale/release (Act III)

### PROMPT ARCHITECTURE

**Core Prompt Template:**
```
60-second brand film, [emotional genre: inspirational/tender/aspirational/defiant], 
Act I (0–15s): [world/character/stakes] established with [atmospheric/visual] language, 
Act II (15–40s): [conflict/escalation/discovery] complicating the setup with [reversal/twist], 
Act III (40–60s): [transformation/resolution] delivering emotional payoff with [brand integration], 
brand philosophy: [core value] woven throughout rather than product-pushed, 
[cinematic color palette] unifying visual language, 
[original music/score] carrying emotional arc, 
premium brand film production quality, 
three-act micro-structure with cathartic ending
```

**Negative Prompts:**
```
product demo disguised as story, late brand tacked-on ending, 
flat second act repeating first act, no emotional payoff, 
feature list instead of values, generic stock footage montage, 
no narrative reversal, talking head corporate video, 
slow pacing throughout, no Act II escalation, 
brand logo as only integration point
```

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Origin Myth**
- Act I: Founder/craftsman in humble setting, obsessed with detail
- Act II: Obstacles, rejection, persistence through difficulty
- Act III: Breakthrough moment; brand emerges as result of journey
- Emotional: Struggle → triumph; authenticity through adversity
- Best For: Heritage brands, artisan products, startups with founder story

**2. The User Transformation**
- Act I: User in relatable "before" state (uncertain, limited, ordinary)
- Act II: Discovery of brand/product; experimentation and small wins
- Act III: Confident "after" state; user becomes best version of self
- Emotional: Insecurity → confidence; brand as catalyst
- Best For: Fitness, beauty, education, professional services

**3. The World Vision**
- Act I: Problem in the world (environmental, social, cultural)
- Act II: Brand's unique approach to solving; philosophy in action
- Act III: Better world emerging; community, nature, future
- Emotional: Concern → hope; brand as movement
- Best For: Sustainability, social enterprise, tech-for-good, B2B mission

**4. The Craft Obsession**
- Act I: Extreme close-ups of material, process, hands at work
- Act II: Complications, perfectionism, time passing
- Act III: Finished product revealed in full beauty; brand as result
- Emotional: Tension → release; appreciation of quality
- Best For: Luxury, automotive, watchmaking, fashion, gastronomy

**5. The Human Connection**
- Act I: Isolation, distance, digital alienation
- Act II: Technology or product bridging gap; first connection
- Act III: Reunion, community, shared moment
- Emotional: Loneliness → belonging; brand as connector
- Best For: Telecom, social platforms, travel, hospitality, family products

**6. The Defiant Stand**
- Act I: Status quo, conformity, rules that limit
- Act II: Character breaks pattern, challenges norm, takes risk
- Act III: Liberation, new possibility, brand as enabler of freedom
- Emotional: Frustration → empowerment; brand as rebellion
- Best For: Youth brands, sports, fashion, creative tools, beverages

### 60-SECOND THREE-ACT BEAT CHART
| Time | Act | Beat | Purpose |
|------|-----|------|---------|
| 0–5s | I | Opening Image | World/character hook |
| 5–15s | I | Stakes | Why we care |
| 15–25s | II | Inciting Incident | Problem/change enters |
| 25–35s | II | Escalation | Rising action, reversal |
| 35–45s | II | Darkest Hour | Maximum tension |
| 45–50s | III | Climax | Transformation moment |
| 50–55s | III | Brand Integration | Organic revelation |
| 55–60s | III | Resolution | Catharsis, final image |

### EXAMPLE PROMPTS

**Craft Obsession Brand Film:**
> 60-second brand film for artisan leather goods, Act I opens with extreme macro close-ups of weathered hands selecting full-grain hide from stack, warm workshop light revealing leather texture and tool patina in 5-second atmospheric shots, Act II shows craftsman cutting, stitching, burnishing with escalating complications — needle breaks, thread tangles, leather resists — paced at 2-second cuts building tension, Act III at 45 seconds reveals finished briefcase in perfect golden light as craftsman places it on oak table, camera pulling back to show workshop filled with decades of work, brand logo embossed on leather revealed organically in final 5 seconds with tagline "Time takes time", cinematic warm amber color palette throughout, premium brand film production, three-act structure with cathartic craft payoff

**Human Connection Brand Film:**
> 60-second brand film for telecommunications company, Act I shows elderly woman alone in apartment with rain on window, cool blue isolation palette, 4-second slow shots establishing loneliness, Act II begins at 15 seconds with granddaughter's face appearing on tablet screen — connection established, warmth entering frame, escalating to video calls with whole family, virtual birthday party, doctor consultation, paced at accelerating 2–3 second cuts, Act III at 45 seconds shows woman teaching online watercolor class to grandchildren from her sunny apartment now filled with plants and light, camera pulling back through window to reveal city of connected windows glowing warm at dusk, brand logo appears as natural part of interface in final 5 seconds, emotional arc from isolation to belonging, cinematic color shift from blue to gold across 60 seconds

### TECHNICAL NOTES FOR AI GENERATION
- Specify "60-second brand film" for format context
- Break prompt into "Act I/II/III" explicitly
- Include "emotional arc" descriptor ("isolation to belonging")
- Mention "brand philosophy" or "values" rather than "product features"
- Use "cathartic ending" or "emotional payoff" for resolution
- Include "premium production" or "cinematic" for quality signal
- Specify color palette shifts across acts if relevant
- Mention "organic brand integration" to prevent tacked-on logo look
