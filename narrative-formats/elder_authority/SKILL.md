# SKILL: Elder & Authority
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Character & Direction System

---

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of cinematographic techniques that honor age, experience, and institutional authority. Combines gravitas lighting that reveals the topography of lived experience with compositional strategies that signal wisdom, earned respect, and the weight of time. Avoids patronizing softness or dehumanizing distance — instead frames elders and authority figures with the dignity of full personhood.

### TRIGGER KEYWORDS
elder, elderly, old man, old woman, authority figure, wise person, judge, professor, patriarch, matriarch, aged face, wrinkles, gravitas, experienced, dignity, aging, senior character

### CORE RULES
- Lighting reveals character, not flatters youth: side light and texture are appropriate and respectful
- Eye-level or slight low angle signals respect and authority — avoid high angle (makes subject small)
- Wrinkles, texture, and lived-in features are expressive assets — do not soften them away
- Stillness signals authority: elders hold positions with certainty, not restlessness
- Hands are particularly expressive for elders: weathered, capable, deliberate
- Framing should give subject adequate breathing room — isolation within the frame signals weight
- Warm key light suggests wisdom and warmth; cool light suggests distance or institutional power

---

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**Gravitas Lighting:**
- Key Light: Soft but directional — reveals texture without cruelty
- Wrinkles as Map: Lighting should reveal wrinkles as topography of experience, not flaws to hide
- Eye Light: Critical — catchlights bring life and alertness to aging eyes
- Shadow Fill: Gentle fill (1:2 to 1:4 ratio) preserving dimension without harsh contrast
- Warmth: Slightly warmer key (3200K–4000K) suggests warmth, memory, hearth
- Avoid: Hard overhead light that creates deep eye sockets; flat beauty light that erases character

**Detail Honoring:**
- Hands: Always include hands when possible — veins, spots, arthritis = labor, time, skill
- Texture: Skin pores, fabric weave of well-worn clothing, patina on objects held
- Objects: Glasses, cane, watch, book, tool — extensions of identity and history
- Environment: Personal space filled with evidence of life (books, photos, tools, mementos)
- Movement: Slower, deliberate gestures; frame should accommodate this pacing
- Voice Implied: Visuals suggest cadence — stillness, breath, measured movement

**Wisdom Signaling — Composition:**
- Centered Stability: Subject centered or in stable thirds position; no dynamic Dutch angles
- Eye Level or Slight Low: Equal or slight elevation; never looking down on elder
- Background Depth: Environment visible behind subject suggesting life history (library, workshop, garden)
- Seated Power: Seated position with upright posture = throne, desk, armchair authority
- Standing Dignity: If standing, full body visible with strong posture; no leaning unless narratively justified
- Framing Space: Generous headroom and leading room = respect, breathing room, unhurried

**Institutional Authority:**
- Scale: Subject framed with institutional architecture (columns, bookshelves, desks, flags)
- Symmetry: Centered, balanced composition = order, tradition, establishment
- Costume: Robes, uniform, suit, or traditional dress = role and position
- Props: Gavel, pen, stethoscope, book, medal = tools of authority
- Light: Often cooler (4000K–5000K) for institutional; warmer for personal wisdom
- Depth: Deep focus keeping subject and institutional background sharp

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Wisdom Portrait**
- Setup: Seated in environment filled with life evidence
- Light: Window light from side, warm, soft, revealing texture
- Framing: Medium shot or medium close-up with hands visible
- Background: Bookshelves, workshop tools, garden — depth of life
- Emotional: Accumulated knowledge, peace, perspective, acceptance
- Best For: Documentary, biography, educational, memorial

**2. The Institutional Seat**
- Setup: Behind desk, on bench, at podium, in chair of office
- Light: Controlled, often cooler; institutional but not cruel
- Framing: Centered, symmetrical, subject in seat of power
- Background: Architecture of institution — wood paneling, flags, seals, books
- Props: Tools of role — gavel, pen, glasses, document
- Emotional: Authority earned or bestowed, tradition, judgment, leadership
- Best For: Legal, political, academic, religious, corporate

**3. The Craftsman's Hands**
- Setup: Close-up or medium shot focused on hands at work
- Light: Task light or window light illuminating hands and object
- Detail: Veins, spots, calluses, precision of movement
- Object: Tool, instrument, book, material being worked
- Emotional: Skill, time, mastery, transmission of knowledge
- Best For: Documentary, artisan profiles, educational, legacy stories

**4. The Generational Bridge**
- Setup: Two-shot or medium wide with elder and younger person
- Framing: Equal eye levels; both given respectful space
- Touch: Hand on shoulder, passing object, shared activity
- Light: Warm, connecting both subjects
- Emotional: Transmission, mentorship, continuity, hope
- Best For: Family, education, tradition, community stories

**5. The Contemplative Silence**
- Setup: Subject alone, looking out window or at object
- Movement: Minimal; stillness as presence
- Light: Natural, time-of-day specific (morning = reflection; evening = legacy)
- Framing: Generous space around subject; time moves slowly
- Emotional: Memory, acceptance, solitude as fullness rather than loneliness
- Best For: Drama, documentary, philosophical content, end-of-life stories

**6. The Active Elder**
- Setup: Subject engaged in physical activity — walking, gardening, dancing, exercising
- Framing: Full body or medium wide showing capability
- Light: Bright, energetic, outdoor or active interior
- Movement: Respecting actual pace but showing vitality
- Emotional: Vitality, refusal to be defined by age, continued engagement
- Best For: Health, lifestyle, inspirational, sports

- Include "generous headroom" and "leading room" for respectful framing
- Specify "seated upright" or "standing dignity" for posture
- Use "background depth" with specific environment (library, workshop)
- Avoid "soft focus beauty" — specify "sharp detail" or "texture revealing"
