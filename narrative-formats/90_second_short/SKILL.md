# SKILL: 90-Second Short
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Genre & Format Expertise

---

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of the 90-second short film — the maximum duration for true narrative completeness in micro-form. Allows genuine character establishment, conflict development, and resolution beats while maintaining the compression discipline of short-form. Research on mini-documentaries and micro-dramas shows 90 seconds can support a full arc if every beat earns its place [^40^][^42^][^48^].

---

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**Character Establishment (0–20s):**
- Opening Image: First 5 seconds must show character in defining action or environment [^40^]
- Visual Hook: Motion, color, or sound that intrigues before dialogue [^48^]
- Trait Revelation: Show, don't tell — one action reveals core character trait
- Want/Need: Audience must understand what character wants by second 15
- World Rules: Establish setting and its rules (physical, social, magical) in first 20 seconds

**Conflict Development (20–55s):**
- Inciting Incident: Event that disrupts status quo at 20–25 second mark [^40^]
- Rising Action: 2–3 escalating complications or obstacles
- Debate/Midpoint: Character faces choice or realization at 40–45 seconds [^40^]
- Second Turning Point: Intensification at 50–55 seconds leading to climax [^40^]
- Beat Density: ~5 plot beats in this section; one every 7 seconds [^42^]

**Resolution Beats (55–90s):**
- Climax: Highest tension at 60–70 seconds [^40^]
- Aftermath: Immediate consequence or emotional shift (10 seconds)
- Resolution: New status quo or understanding (5–10 seconds)
- Final Image: Last frame echoes opening image but transformed [^40^]
- Emotional Payoff: Moment that lingers — smile, gesture, hope [^48^]

**Pacing Discipline:**
- Setup: Slower, 4–6 second shots for world-building
- Conflict: Accelerating, 2–4 second shots for tension
- Climax: Rapid, 1–3 second shots for peak intensity
- Resolution: Slowing, 4–6 second shots for catharsis
- One-Thought-Per-Shot: Each cut advances emotion or understanding [^48^]

```
55–70s: [climax] at highest tension, 
70–90s: [resolution] with emotional payoff and [transformed final image], 
[opening image] echoed but transformed in final frame [^40^], 
[sound design/music] carrying emotional precision, 
purposeful editing with one-thought-per-shot discipline [^48^],

```

**Negative Prompts:**
```
no character establishment, inciting incident too late, 
unresolved conflict, flat middle section, 
no emotional payoff, rushed ending, 
exposition through dialogue, multiple characters without focus, 
no visual transformation from opening to closing, 
static camera throughout, no sound design
```

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Full Circle**
- Opening: Character performs action in one emotional state
- Middle: Journey changes character through conflict
- End: Same action performed again but transformed by experience
- Emotional: Growth visible through repetition
- Best For: Character study, redemption, coming-of-age

**2. The Reversal Short**
- Setup: Character appears to be one thing (hero/victim/villain)
- Middle: Evidence accumulates suggesting opposite
- Climax: True nature revealed in surprising moment
- End: Audience recontextualizes entire 90 seconds
- Emotional: Surprise, re-evaluation, moral complexity
- Best For: Thriller, dark comedy, social commentary

**3. The Object Journey**
- Setup: Object introduced with emotional weight (letter, toy, tool)
- Middle: Object passes through hands, changes meaning
- Climax: Object returns to origin transformed
- End: Object's final state reveals story's truth
- Emotional: Circularity, legacy, time
- Best For: Drama, family, historical, sentimental

**4. The Single Location Pressure Cooker**
- Setup: One room, one character (or two), clear need
- Middle: Obstacles within confined space escalate
- Climax: Space itself becomes antagonist or ally
- End: Escape, acceptance, or transformation within same space
- Emotional: Claustrophobia, intimacy, limitation as truth
- Best For: Drama, horror, comedy, experimental

**5. The Silent Film**
- Setup: No dialogue; story told through action and expression
- Middle: Visual metaphors carry narrative weight
- Climax: Pure visual storytelling at peak intensity
- End: Final image communicates everything
- Emotional: Universal, primal, cinematic purity
- Best For: Art film, festival circuit, emotional content, global audience

**6. The Parallel Action**
- Setup: Two characters in separate locations with parallel needs
- Middle: Cuts between both; actions mirror or contrast
- Climax: Paths converge or diverge meaningfully
- End: Connection or separation resolves both arcs
- Emotional: Fate, coincidence, human connection across distance
- Best For: Romance, drama, thriller, social commentary

### 90-SECOND BEAT SHEET
| Seconds | Beat | Narrative Function | Visual Approach |
|---------|------|-------------------|-----------------|
| 0–5 | Opening Image | Hook + world | Motion, color, defining action [^48^] |
| 5–15 | Character | Trait + want | Show, don't tell |
| 15–20 | Status Quo | Normal world | Routine, rhythm, environment |
| 20–25 | Inciting Incident | Disruption | Visual shock, change enters [^40^] |
| 25–35 | Rising Action | Complication 1 | Escalating tension |
| 35–45 | Midpoint | Debate/realization | Choice moment, mirror [^40^] |
| 45–55 | Rising Action | Complication 2 | Peak pressure |
| 55–65 | Climax | Highest tension | Rapid cuts, maximum stakes [^40^] |
| 65–75 | Aftermath | Consequence | Breath, reaction, shift |
| 75–85 | Resolution | New normal | Transformed world |
| 85–90 | Final Image | Echo + transformation | Bookend to opening [^40^] |

### EXAMPLE PROMPTS

**Full Circle Character Study:**
> 90-second dramatic short film, opening image at 0–5 seconds shows elderly man sitting alone on park bench crumpling empty lunch bag with resigned expression, character established as lonely routine-driven widower through 4–6 second slow shots of bench, pigeons, watch-checking, inciting incident at 22 seconds as young mother sits nearby with crying toddler, rising action through 25–55 seconds showing man's initial annoyance escalating to quiet intervention — offering paper airplane from crumpled bag, making funny faces, building paper bird, midpoint at 42 seconds shows mother smiling gratefully and child laughing, climax at 60 seconds with child hugging man's leg and mother inviting him to walk with them, resolution 70–85 seconds showing trio walking away together as man performs same crumpling motion with bag but now smiling, final image at 85–90 seconds echoes opening bench shot but now empty with three sets of footprints leading away, warm golden afternoon light throughout, purposeful editing with one-thought-per-shot, cinematic short film quality

**Reversal Thriller:**
> 90-second thriller short film, opening image 0–5 seconds shows nervous woman checking locks on apartment door at night, character established as paranoid victim through peephole checks, window locking, phone clutching in 4-second tense shots, inciting incident at 20 seconds with knock at door and man's voice claiming to be building maintenance, rising action 25–50 seconds with escalating fear — peephole shows tool belt, woman calls building security who confirms no maintenance scheduled, midpoint at 45 seconds woman grabs kitchen knife and approaches door with determination, climax at 60 seconds as she opens door to reveal... actual maintenance man with legitimate ID and apologetic smile about unscheduled leak check, aftermath 65–75 seconds showing her embarrassed relief and apology, resolution 75–85 seconds as she closes door and laughs at herself, final image 85–90 seconds shows her turning off lights to sleep, camera pulling back through window to reveal same "maintenance man" watching from street below holding different tool belt — opening image of paranoia echoed but transformed into justified fear, cool blue night lighting shifting to warm interior then back to cold exterior, purposeful thriller editing
