# SKILL: 30-Second TV Spot
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Genre & Format Expertise

---

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of the classic broadcast advertising unit that remains the gold standard for brand storytelling. Thirty seconds allows exactly enough time for a compressed narrative arc — problem, solution, payoff — while demanding ruthless efficiency. Research shows 30-second spots outperform shorter formats on conversion metrics and represent the best balance of storytelling and viewer patience [^47^].

---

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**Narrative Arc Compression:**
- Structure: Problem (0–8s) → Solution/Tension (8–20s) → Payoff/CTA (20–30s)
- Problem: Establish relatable friction or desire in first 8 seconds
- Development: Build toward solution or climax in middle 12 seconds
- Resolution: Brand reveal, product benefit, or call-to-action in final 10 seconds
- Emotional Compression: One emotional journey (frustration→relief, desire→satisfaction)
- No Subplots: Every frame must serve the single narrative thread

**Brand Reveal Timing:**
- Early Reveal (0–5s): For established brands with high awareness; logo up front [^45^]
- Mid Reveal (12–18s): For story-driven spots; brand emerges as solution
- Late Reveal (20–25s): For suspense or emotional build; brand as payoff punchline
- Final Frame Logo: Mandatory 3–5 second end card with brand lockup
- Product Visibility: Product must be clearly visible for minimum 5 seconds total

**Attention Retention Data:**
- Dwell Time: Less than 14 seconds of engaged attention in 30-second spot [^45^]
- Drop-off: Viewer attention declines sharply after 15 seconds if not emotionally engaged
- Hook Requirement: Strong emotion must appear in first 5 seconds [^45^]
- Brand Recall: Late brand reveal without early emotional hook = poor recall [^45^]
- Completion Rate: 30-second spots maintain higher completion than 60s on CTV [^47^]

**Pacing & Rhythm:**
- Cut Rate: Average 8–15 cuts in 30 seconds (1 cut per 2–4 seconds)
- First 5 Seconds: Maximum visual density to arrest attention
- Middle 20 Seconds: Breathing room for story; slightly longer takes
- Final 5 Seconds: Rapid recap or logo animation
- Audio Bed: Music or voiceover must carry consistent emotional tone

**Format Specs:**
- Aspect Ratio: 16:9 horizontal (primary); 9:16 vertical for CTV/mobile
- Resolution: 1080p or 4K
- Audio: Mixed for broadcast loudness standards (–24 LKFS)
- End Card: 3–5 second brand lockup with tagline

### PROMPT ARCHITECTURE

**Core Prompt Template:**
```
30-second TV commercial, [genre: comedy/drama/action/emotional], 
frame 1–3: [strong emotional hook] arresting attention immediately, 
seconds 4–12: [problem/frustration/desire] establishing relatable tension, 
seconds 13–22: [solution/building action] developing toward payoff, 
seconds 23–27: [brand reveal/product hero] delivering promise, 
seconds 28–30: [end card/logo/tagline] locking brand memory, 
[cut rate: fast/moderate] pacing with [emotional tone] music bed, 
cinematic commercial quality, 
broadcast standard composition
```

**Negative Prompts:**
```
slow burn setup beyond 5 seconds, multiple storylines, 
brand reveal too early without context, product absence until final frame, 
flat emotional tone, generic stock footage aesthetic, 
no clear problem or solution, ambiguous call-to-action, 
15-second pacing stretched to 30, talking head only, 
cluttered frame without focus
```

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Problem-Solution Classic**
- 0–5s: Relatable problem (spilled coffee, missed bus, bad hair day)
- 6–15s: Problem escalates with comedic or dramatic tension
- 16–22s: Product enters as solution
- 23–27s: Transformation/result
- 28–30s: Logo + tagline
- Emotional: Frustration → relief
- Best For: CPG, household products, services

**2. The Emotional Payoff**
- 0–5s: Quiet emotional moment (child's drawing, old photograph, sunrise)
- 6–18s: Story builds with subtle product integration
- 19–25s: Emotional climax with brand as enabler
- 26–30s: Logo with emotional music swell
- Emotional: Nostalgia → hope
- Best For: Insurance, automotive, telecom, holiday campaigns

**3. The Comedy Punchline**
- 0–8s: Setup with normal situation
- 9–18s: Absurd escalation or unexpected twist
- 19–25s: Product as punchline delivery
- 26–30s: Logo + final laugh
- Emotional: Amusement → surprise
- Best For: Snacks, beverages, entertainment, apps

**4. The Product Hero**
- 0–5s: Product in isolation, beautiful, desirable
- 6–20s: Product in use, demonstrating benefit
- 21–27s: Result/satisfaction
- 28–30s: Logo + product shot
- Emotional: Desire → satisfaction
- Best For: Luxury, tech, automotive, fashion

**5. The Before/After Montage**
- 0–3s: "Before" state (messy, slow, ugly)
- 4–18s: Product application montage (fast cuts)
- 19–25s: "After" state (clean, fast, beautiful)
- 26–30s: Logo + split-screen comparison
- Emotional: Dissatisfaction → aspiration
- Best For: Cleaning, beauty, fitness, renovation

**6. The Testimonial Slice**
- 0–5s: Real person in authentic environment
- 6–20s: Story of problem and discovery
- 21–27s: Product reveal with genuine reaction
- 28–30s: Logo + credibility line
- Emotional: Skepticism → trust
- Best For: Health, financial, educational, B2B
