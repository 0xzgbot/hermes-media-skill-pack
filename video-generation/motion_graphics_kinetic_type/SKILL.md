# SKILL: Motion Graphics & Kinetic Typography
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Animated Information Design

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of motion graphics, kinetic typography, and animated data visualization for AI-generated campaigns. This skill encodes the grammar of graphic animation — how text, shapes, and data move through time to communicate information with emotional impact. Essential for title sequences, explainer animations, social media text animations, logo reveals, and data-driven storytelling.

---

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**The Motion Graphics Pipeline:**
- **Design:** Vector-clean aesthetic, deliberate color system, grid-based composition
- **Animation:** Easing curves (ease-in, ease-out, ease-in-out), anticipation, follow-through
- **Typography:** Type as image — scale, weight, and motion carry meaning
- **Timing:** Holds, pauses, and beats synced to message rhythm
- **Sound Correlation:** Motion synchronized to audio hits, beats, and swells

**Kinetic Typography Categories:**
- **Sequential Reveal:** Words appear one by one, building meaning progressively
- **Expressive Scale:** Key words explode in size for emphasis
- **Path Animation:** Text flows along curves, roads, or trajectory lines
- **3D Space Typography:** Words exist in depth, rotating and shifting perspective
- **Texture Fill:** Text filled with video, gradients, or animated patterns
- **Mask Reveal:** Text revealed through wipes, shapes, or camera movement

**Motion Principles (12 Principles Adapted):**
- **Squash & Stretch:** Text and shapes deform on impact and acceleration
- **Anticipation:** Small backward motion before forward action
- **Staging:** Single clear idea per frame; no visual competition
- **Follow-Through:** Elements overshoot and settle; nothing stops abruptly
- **Slow In / Slow Out:** Easing makes motion feel physical, not mechanical
- **Arcs:** Natural motion follows curves, not straight lines
- **Secondary Action:** Supporting elements animate to reinforce primary message
- **Timing:** More frames = slower/heavier; fewer frames = faster/lighter

**Data Visualization Animation:**
- **Bar Charts:** Bars grow from baseline with staggered timing
- **Line Graphs:** Lines draw themselves left-to-right revealing trend
- **Pie Charts:** Slices unfurl from center like opening flower
- **Numbers:** Count-up animation for statistics; decimal precision for credibility
- **Maps:** Paths trace routes; regions illuminate sequentially
- **Timelines:** Events pop into existence along horizontal axis

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Title Sequence**
- Typography: Bold sans-serif, large scale, high contrast
- Motion: Words enter from off-screen, lock into grid, hold, exit
- Timing: Synced to music tempo; hits on downbeats
- Background: Abstract shapes, brand colors, subtle gradient motion
- Best For: Film/TV openings, campaign launches, event intros

**2. The Explainer Graphic**
- Structure: Problem → Process → Solution
- Icons: Simple geometric representations of concepts
- Motion: Icons assemble, transform, and resolve
- Text: Short phrases, large scale, sequential reveal
- Best For: Product explanations, educational content, process visualization

**3. The Social Media Text Pop**
- Format: Vertical 9:16, centered text
- Animation: Word-by-word pop with slight overshoot and settle
- Color: High contrast, brand-aligned, thumb-stopping
- Duration: 2–4 seconds per text card
- Best For: Instagram Reels, TikTok text overlays, quote graphics

**4. The Logo Reveal**
- Build-Up: Abstract shapes resolve into logo geometry
- Motion: Elements converge from multiple directions
- Timing: Anticipation → action → hold → resolve
- Sound Visual: Motion aligns with brand sonic identity
- Best For: Brand films, campaign openers, product launches

**5. The Data Story**
- Numbers: Large, central, count-up animation
- Context: Supporting bars, lines, or icons animate around statistic
- Progression: Multiple data points reveal in sequence
- Credibility: Source citation, precise decimals, clean labels
- Best For: Annual reports, social proof, infographic videos

### TECHNICAL NOTES FOR AI GENERATION
- Use "motion graphics" or "kinetic typography" as primary style signal
- Specify vector-clean, flat design, or geometric to avoid photorealism
- Mention easing curves: "ease-in-out", "elastic overshoot", "smooth deceleration"
- For text animations: specify "sequential reveal", "word-by-word", or "character animation"
- For FLUX: describe motion in static terms — "words frozen mid-animation", "kinetic type frame"
- For LTX: describe motion explicitly — "text sliding into frame", "icons assembling"
- Color systems should be limited (2–3 colors) for graphic design coherence
