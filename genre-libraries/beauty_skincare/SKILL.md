# SKILL: Beauty & Skincare
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Brand Voice & Style

---

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of the dual visual language that balances softness and transformation with clinical trust and scientific credibility. Beauty cinematography must simultaneously suggest dreamlike metamorphosis and medical-grade reliability. The face is both canvas and patient; the product is both poetry and chemistry. Every frame navigates the tension between desire and trust.

### TRIGGER KEYWORDS
beauty, skincare, cosmetics, makeup, face cream, serum, beauty product, beauty shoot, skin, glow, luminous, clean beauty, beauty commercial, product beauty, face shot, skin texture, before and after

### CORE RULES
- Skin is the canvas: lighting must flatter while showing texture — clamshell softbox standard
- Product packaging requires separate lighting consideration from skin/face
- Glow is achieved through rim light + internal luminosity descriptors, not overexposure
- Clinical trust requires cool, precise framing; aspirational desire requires warm, soft framing — choose based on brand
- Macro detail of product (serum drop, cream texture) builds desire through intimate specificity
- Diversity in skin tone representation requires individual lighting setup per tone — never one-size approach
- Before/after convention: before = flat neutral light; after = flattering warm light

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**Softness & Transformation:**
- Light: Ultra-soft, wrapping, diffused — large sources, bounce, silk, softbox
- Skin Rendering: Pore-level detail visible but flattering; no harsh texture emphasis
- Color: Warm, desaturated, pastel — rose, peach, cream, soft gold
- Focus: Sharp on eyes and lips; gentle falloff on skin
- Motion: Slow, fluid — cream spreading, droplet falling, hair moving in wind
- Macro: Extreme close-ups of product texture, skin absorption, ingredient beauty
- Transformation Visual: Before/after, time-lapse glow, morning light progression

**Clinical Trust:**
- Light: Clean, even, precise — ring light, beauty dish, or medical-grade LED
- Background: White, light gray, or clinical blue; minimal and sterile
- Props: Lab equipment, ingredients, molecular structures, white coats, gloves
- Typography: Clean sans-serif; data points, percentages, clinical study references
- Color: White, silver, pale blue, transparent — suggesting purity and science
- Framing: Symmetrical, centered, measured — no accidental composition
- Focus: Razor-sharp throughout; deep focus for lab and product detail

**The Balance — Soft Clinical:**
- Light: Soft but bright; warm but even
- Environment: Clean but not cold; clinical but with natural elements (plant, water, stone)
- Product: Shown in both "dream" context (floating, glowing) and "real" context (bathroom, hand, face)
- Talent: Diverse skin tones, real skin texture, no uncanny perfection
- Color: Neutral base with soft warmth; never harsh or cold

**Ingredient Obsession:**
- Macro: Droplets, textures, botanicals, molecules, crystals
- Light: Backlit for transparency; side-lit for texture
- Movement: Pouring, mixing, activating, absorbing
- Color: Natural ingredient colors — green, gold, clear, white
- Purity: Single ingredient hero shots; no clutter

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Morning Glow**
- Setup: Subject in soft morning light performing skincare routine
- Light: Window light through sheer curtain; 4000K–4500K soft and warm
- Action: Applying product, water splashing, looking in mirror
- Skin: Natural texture visible; healthy glow, not artificial shine
- Environment: Clean bathroom or bedroom; white, marble, wood, plant
- Emotional: Renewal, self-care, ritual, beginning
- Best For: Daily skincare, cleansers, moisturizers, SPF

**2. The Ingredient Cathedral**
- Setup: Single ingredient as hero — botanical, mineral, or molecular
- Camera: Macro or probe lens; extreme detail
- Light: Backlit for transparency; or dramatic spot for preciousness
- Movement: Slow motion — drop falling into water, cream spreading, crystal forming
- Background: Pure black or white; no distraction
- Emotional: Purity, science, nature, preciousness
- Best For: Serums, active ingredients, natural brands, luxury skincare

**3. The Clinical Promise**
- Setup: Lab or clinical environment with product and evidence
- Light: Even, bright, 5000K–5500K; no shadows
- Props: Beakers, microscopes, white coats, data screens, before/after charts
- Talent: Diverse subjects in clinical setting; real skin, real results
- Framing: Symmetrical, clean, informative
- Emotional: Trust, evidence, science, credibility
- Best For: Medical skincare, dermatology, anti-aging, problem-solution

**4. The Transformation Timeline**
- Setup: Time-lapse or sequence showing skin improvement
- Light: Matched exactly across time points
- Framing: Same angle, same distance, same subject
- Progression: Days 1, 7, 14, 30 — subtle but visible improvement
- Emotional: Hope, patience, proof, aspiration
- Best For: Treatment products, acne, anti-aging, brightening

**5. The Sensory Cream**
- Setup: Product texture as protagonist
- Camera: Macro, overhead, or 45-degree
- Action: Finger dipping into jar, cream spreading on skin, absorption
- Light: Soft, warm, revealing texture without harshness
- Sound Visual: Rich, creamy, melting, absorbing
- Emotional: Luxury, indulgence, tactile desire, self-love
- Best For: Moisturizers, masks, luxury creams, body care

**6. The Diverse Spectrum**
- Setup: Multiple subjects across skin tone spectrum
- Light: Carefully balanced for all tones; no ashy or blown highlights
- Framing: Equal, respectful, celebratory
- Product: Shown working on all skin types
- Emotional: Inclusivity, representation, universal beauty, belonging
- Best For: Modern inclusive brands, foundation, SPF, universal products
