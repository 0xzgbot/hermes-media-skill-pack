# Beauty & Cosmetics Cinematography

## Core Philosophy

Beauty cinematography exists in the tension between perfection and relatability. The skin must look achievable yet aspirational. The product must appear luxurious yet accessible. Light is the primary tool for navigating this paradox.

## Skin Tone Preservation

### Color Science Fundamentals
- **Reference target**: Use a ColorChecker Classic or Digital SG at the start of every setup. The 18% gray card must read true under your final light mixture.
- **Color temperature**: Match source to intent. 3200K (tungsten) for warmth and intimacy. 5600K (daylight) for clinical skincare and sun care. Variable 4000-4500K for modern "natural" beauty.
- **Mixed light trap**: Never mix uncontrolled daylight with tungsten on skin. The dual-color shadow model destroys complexion nuance.
- **LED quality**: Only high-CRI (95+) and full-spectrum sources. Low-CRI LEDs create spectral spikes that exaggerate blemishes and discoloration.

### Exposure for Skin
- **Metering point**: Caucasian skin typically +1 to +1.3 stops above middle gray. Medium brown skin at or slightly above middle gray. Deep skin: protect highlights; allow rich shadows, but maintain separation in hair and clothing.
- **Never clip**: A clipped pore is a permanent loss of texture. Protect highlights at the expense of background exposure.
- **The "glow" zone**: Healthy skin lives in the upper midtones. Lift slightly and roll off highlights gently.

### Ethnic Skin Considerations
- **South Asian / Middle Eastern**: Often strong olive undertone that can shift muddy under tungsten. Test and adjust with white balance or minus-green gel.
- **East Asian**: porcelain to golden undertones; careful with cool light that creates sallow reading.
- **African / Afro-Caribbean**: Deep skin rewards rich, dimensional light. Avoid flat frontal that eliminates sculpting. Embrace specularity on cheekbones and brow.
- **Latine / Mixed**: Warm undertones respond beautifully to golden hour sources; avoid magenta casts.

## Softbox Ratio Architecture

### The Key-to-Fill System

| Ratio | Stop Difference | Mood / Application |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| 1:1 | 0 stops | Flat, clean, studio backdrop, ingredient focus |
| 2:1 | 1 stop | Soft beauty, natural daylight feel, tutorials |
| 3:1 | 1.5 stops | Glamorous, dimensional, standard beauty campaign |
| 4:1 | 2 stops | Dramatic, editorial, chiaroscuro glamour |
| 8:1+ | 3+ stops | High drama, noir, sculptural, artistic |

### Softbox Size and Distance
- **Beauty dish (20-30cm)**: Crisp, punchy, defined shadow edge. Classic for fashion beauty. Creates circular catchlight.
- **Small softbox (60x60cm)**: Controlled, portable, moderate wrap. Good for product highlights.
- **Medium softbox (90x120cm)**: Versatile beauty standard. Soft shadow with shape retention.
- **Large softbox / Octa (120-150cm)**: Extremely soft, enveloping, forgiving. The modern beauty standard.
- **Book light (frame + diffusion)**: Maximum softness, zero specular aggression. For mature skin or extreme close-up.

### Position Vocabulary
- **45/45 classic**: 45 degrees to side, 45 degrees elevated. The portrait standard. Sculpts cheekbone, defines jaw.
- **Butterfly / Paramount**: Centered above, pointed down 30-45 degrees. Creates shadow under nose (butterfly). Glamour, 1940s legacy.
- **Loop**: Similar to butterfly but shifted slightly to one side. Loop shadow from nose falls onto cheek. Most universally flattering.
- **Rembrandt**: Far side light, 45+ degrees, elevated. Triangle of light on far cheek. Dramatic, painterly.
- **Split**: 90 degrees to side. Half face lit, half shadow. Maximum drama.
- **Cross-light / backlight**: Light from behind subject. Rim and separation. Used with front fill for ethereal glow.

## Product Highlight Control

### Bottle and Jar Language
- **Glass**: Needs gradient highlight to read as transparent. A thin bright line (edge light) defines the form.
- **Frosted glass / opaque**: Soft wrap to show shape; gradient from highlight to shadow across the surface.
- **Chrome / metallic cap**: Same rules as automotive chrome: composed reflections only.
- **Pump and actuator**: Must read as functional. Small accent light on the mechanism.

### Texture and Material Shots
- **Cream / lotion**: Spread on non-porous surface (glass or acrylic). Side light reveals texture and peak structure.
- **Powder**: Cloud and settle shots require high-speed; 120fps minimum for visible particle separation.
- **Serum droplet**: Macro with controlled gradient behind. The "perfect drop" is a craft specialty.
- **Swatch on skin**: Even, soft light. No specular on the product itself—let the color read true.

### Liquid and Splash
- **High viscosity (oil, serum)**: Slow movement, glossy surface. Single sharp source for specular line.
- **Low viscosity (water, toner)**: Fast splash, requires high-speed. Backlight or strong side light for edge definition.
- **Foam / mousse**: Air cell structure visible only under raking light. Side or backlight critical.

## Macro and Close-Up Discipline

### Depth of Field Management
- **100mm macro at f/2.8**: 2-3mm depth on a close face. The eyelash sharp, iris soft. Intimate, abstract.
- **100mm macro at f/8-f/11**: Clinical, full face sharp for product claim verification.
- **Probe lenses (Laowa 24mm)**: Insert into product, travel through formula. Reveals viscosity and texture.

### Critical Focus Points
- **Eye campaign**: Focus on the iris pattern, never the lash. Lash blur is acceptable; iris blur is failure.
- **Lip campaign**: Focus on the wet line (the meeting of dry and moist lip). Texture must be visible.
- **Skin texture**: Focus on the plane of maximum interest—usually the cheekbone or forehead center.

## Makeup Application Cinematography

### Tutorial and How-To
- **Over-the-shoulder / POV**: Camera behind artist, seeing what artist sees. 60-100mm equivalent.
- **Side angle**: Reveals face plane and product interaction. 50-85mm.
- **Detail cutaways**: Macro of brush loading, product pick-up, application stroke. 100mm macro.
- **Hand model lighting**: Hands must match face lighting exactly. Test hand skin tone under final lights.

### Motion and Pacing
- **Brush strokes**: Real-time or slight slow-motion (48-60fps). Too fast and the craft is lost; too slow and it becomes tedious.
- **Blend moments**: Speed ramp through the blend; the transformation is the payoff.
- **Reveal**: Locked-off before/after camera position. Identical lighting, identical angle.

## Environmental Context

### Bathroom and Vanity
- **Mirror light**: Practical lights must not blow out; use dimmers or practicals with visible filaments.
- **Tile and surface**: Reflection control essential; every surface is a potential source or reflection.
- **Steam and atmosphere**: Practical or glycerin-based haze. For shower steam, consider low-contrast lighting to maintain product visibility.

### The Lab / Clinical
- **White on white**: Requires edge definition through shadow or subtle color variation in whites.
- **Pipette and beaker**: Clean, sharp, backlit for liquid translucency.
- **Scientist / formulator**: White coat against white background needs separation light from behind.

## Grade Direction

### The Beauty LUT Philosophy
- **Soft rolloff**: No hard clips in highlights or shadows. Creamy transitions.
- **Saturation discipline**: Lip color and product color preserved; background and non-skin elements often desaturated.
- **Skin tone vectors**: Push toward warmth in midtones. Protect against magenta/sunburn cast.
- **Pore and line management**: Moderate frequency separation in post. Never eliminate texture completely—it becomes wax.
- **The "airbrush" line**: When skin looks better but still real. Cross this line and trust is lost.

## Quick Reference: Skin Checklist

Before approving any beauty frame:

- [ ] Skin tone accurate to subject and intent
- [ ] No color casts (green, magenta, orange) in neutral skin zones
- [ ] Texture visible: pores, fine lines, but not emphasized
- [ ] Catchlight in eyes: present, shaped, not blown
- [ ] Product color true to life under final light
- [ ] Highlight roll-off smooth on forehead, cheekbones, nose
- [ ] Neck and chest match face; no mask effect
- [ ] Hands in frame match face tone
- [ ] Background supports, never competes with subject
- [ ] Sharp plane exactly where story demands it
