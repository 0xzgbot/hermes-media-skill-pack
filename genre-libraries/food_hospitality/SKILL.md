# SKILL: Food & Hospitality
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Brand Voice & Style

---

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of the warm, abundant visual language that triggers appetite, comfort, and social connection. Food and hospitality cinematography operates on the primal level — it must make the viewer smell, taste, and feel the temperature of what is on screen. Warmth is not optional; it is the primary emotional currency. Abundance signals generosity; tactile detail triggers mirror neurons.

### TRIGGER KEYWORDS
food, hospitality, restaurant, cuisine, food photography, food shot, meal, dish, plating, steam, appetite, food styling, cooking, farm to table, chef, ingredient, texture food, appetizing

### CORE RULES
- Warmth is non-negotiable: 2700K–3200K lighting for cooked food; cooler for fresh/raw
- Steam indicates temperature and freshness — specify "wisps of steam rising" for hot food
- Texture is the appetite trigger: macro or close angle reveals pores, crust, glaze, drizzle
- Overhead (flatlay) vs. 45-degree angle vs. side-on: each serves different food types
- Background warmth supports foreground subject but doesn't compete with it
- Garnish and sauce placement must be intentional — describe specific placement
- Color contrast drives appetite: complementary colors on plate (red tomato on green herb, etc.)

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**Warmth Architecture:**
- Color Temperature: 2800K–4000K; never cool or clinical unless specific narrative
- Light: Practical sources — fire, candle, window, lamp; warm tungsten dominant
- Color Palette: Amber, gold, brown, cream, tomato red, sage green, warm white
- Skin Rendering: Warm, flushed, healthy; subjects look well-fed and happy
- Environment: Wood, brick, linen, copper, cast iron, ceramic — materials that hold heat
- Atmosphere: Steam, smoke, condensation, sauce gloss — temperature made visible

**Abundance Signaling:**
- Composition: Overflowing, generous, never stingy; food fills frame with generosity
- Layering: Multiple dishes, ingredients, textures stacked and overlapping
- Negative Space: Minimal; frame should feel full and generous
- Group Context: Multiple hands, shared plates, passing dishes — social eating
- Scale: Close-ups that make food monumental; or wide shots of lavish tables
- Repetition: Rows of identical items (oysters, pastries, glasses) = plenty
- The "Hero + Supporting Cast": Main dish central; sides, ingredients, tools surrounding

**Tactile Desire:**
- Macro: Texture of crust, fat marbling, sauce drip, cheese pull, steam curl
- Gloss: Wet surfaces catching light — sauce, oil, glaze, condensation
- Action: Cutting, tearing, pulling, pouring, dipping, lifting — food in motion
- Hands: Human hands touching, breaking, serving food = invitation to viewer
- Sound Visual: Implied crunch, sizzle, pour, crack through motion and steam
- Temperature: Hot food steaming, cold food glistening with condensation

**Hospitality Environment:**
- Restaurant: Warm ambient, candlelight, exposed kitchen, open flame, wood surfaces
- Home Kitchen: Lived-in, functional, messy in beautiful way — flour on counter, wooden spoon
- Bar: Backlit bottles, warm wood, brass, mirror, low ceiling = intimacy
- Outdoor: Long table, natural light, shared plates, wine, landscape
- Service: Staff in motion, plates arriving, glasses being filled — attentive care

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Hero Dish**
- Setup: Single dish as protagonist; everything else supports
- Light: Single warm key from above or side; dark background
- Action: Sauce being poured, garnish falling, steam rising
- Camera: Slow push-in or static with subtle motion
- Detail: Macro of texture, gloss, color layers
- Emotional: Desire, perfection, chef's pride, peak flavor
- Best For: Fine dining, recipe content, restaurant promo, cookbook

**2. The Shared Table**
- Setup: Multiple hands, dishes, glasses in dynamic arrangement
- Camera: Overhead or 45-degree; medium wide
- Action: Passing, serving, toasting, laughing — food as social bond
- Light: Warm ambient; candles, overhead fixtures, window
- Composition: Abundant, overlapping, generous — no empty space
- Emotional: Community, family, celebration, belonging
- Best For: Restaurants, family brands, holidays, events, wine

**3. The Process Romance**
- Setup: Chef or home cook in process of creation
- Camera: Close on hands, ingredients, technique
- Action: Chopping, kneading, stirring, plating — skill made visible
- Environment: Kitchen with evidence of work — flour, steam, used tools
- Light: Warm practicals; fire from stove, oven glow
- Emotional: Craft, tradition, care, transformation
- Best For: Cooking shows, artisan brands, restaurants, education

**4. The Ingredient Portrait**
- Setup: Raw ingredients as beautiful objects
- Camera: Macro or still-life overhead
- Light: Natural or soft studio; revealing freshness
- Arrangement: Artful but natural; farmers market aesthetic
- Texture: Dirt on root vegetables, water on leaves, marbling on meat
- Emotional: Origin, freshness, farm-to-table, natural abundance
- Best For: Organic brands, grocery, farm, market, health food

**5. The Steam & Sizzle**
- Setup: Food being cooked — pan, grill, oven, fire
- Camera: Close on cooking surface; or chef's face illuminated by flame
- Action: Sear, flip, char, bubble, caramelize — transformation through heat
- Light: Fire glow, stove light, oven interior — warm and dramatic
- Atmosphere: Steam, smoke, oil spray, sparks
- Emotional: Power, transformation, alchemy, danger, deliciousness
- Best For: Grilling, steakhouses, cooking content, energy brands

**6. The Late-Night Comfort**
- Setup: Intimate, small-scale food moment — snack, dessert, midnight meal
- Camera: Close, personal, low light
- Environment: Kitchen at night, couch, bed — private comfort
- Light: Single warm source; fridge light, phone screen, lamp
- Action: Quiet eating, savoring, comfort
- Emotional: Solace, indulgence, privacy, self-care
- Best For: Snack foods, delivery, comfort food, dessert, solo dining
