# SKILL: Streetwear & Youth Culture
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Brand Voice & Style

---

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of the raw, unpolished visual language that signals authenticity through subcultural alignment. Streetwear cinematography rejects perfection in favor of energy, immediacy, and cultural code. It speaks the visual dialect of skate parks, basement shows, block parties, and late-night city streets — where legitimacy is earned through presence, not production value [^50^][^51^][^52^].

### TRIGGER KEYWORDS
streetwear, youth culture, street style, urban fashion, skate, hip-hop, sneaker culture, graffiti, raw aesthetic, authentic, subcultural, street photography, youth brand, underground, unpolished, real

### CORE RULES
- Authenticity over perfection: technical imperfection (grain, underexposure, motion blur) is a feature
- Environment is credential: skate parks, basketball courts, rooftops, underpasses — not studios
- Clothing must be precisely observed: specific brands, colorways, styling details signal cultural fluency
- Energy over composition: dynamic, asymmetric, even awkward compositions can feel more real
- Grain and texture are intentional: film grain, high ISO, slightly blown highlights signal documentary truth
- Subject should look at home in their environment: belonging, not posing
- Avoid commercial polish: if it looks like an ad, it has failed its own aesthetic

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**Raw Energy — Production Aesthetic:**
- Camera: Handheld, phone, or action-cam quality; slight shake is feature not bug
- Lighting: Available light only — streetlights, fluorescents, phone screens, flash
- Color: High contrast, saturated, or pushed film stock; no neutral grading
- Cuts: Fast, rhythmic, often on beat; 1–2 seconds per shot in montage
- Sound: Diegetic and loud — city noise, music, shouting, skate wheels, concrete
- Aspect Ratio: Vertical 9:16 native; or 16:9 with lo-fi crop aesthetic
- Quality: Intentionally below cinema — VHS texture, grain, light leaks, digital artifacts

**Lo-Fi Authenticity Signals:**
- UGC Aesthetic: User-generated content look — selfie angles, mirror shots, car interiors
- Imperfect Focus: Slight softness or hunting focus = real-time, unscripted
- Natural Environment: Alleyways, parking garages, subway stations, bedrooms, kitchens
- Unscripted Action: No models walking runways; real skaters falling, real crowds moshing
- Timestamp/Overlay: Fake or real timestamp, location tags, phone UI elements
- Flash Photography: Harsh on-camera flash creating blown highlights and deep shadows

**Subculture Signals:**
- Typography: Bold sans-serif, distressed fonts, graffiti-inspired scripts, hand-drawn lettering [^51^]
- Graphics: Anime references, vintage comic art, political messaging, neighborhood iconography [^51^]
- Fashion Codes: Oversized fits, technical fabrics, layering, vintage mixed with new, DIY distressing [^50^]
- Geography: City-specific visual dialect — Tokyo neon, New York grit, London council estate, LA palm trees [^50^]
- Community: Crew shots, group dynamics, generational layering (OGs and youngers)
- Drop Culture Visuals: Limited release queues, unboxing hype, resale culture, collaboration teasers [^52^]

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Basement Show**
- Setup: Crowded, dark, loud — band or DJ performing to intimate crowd
- Camera: Handheld in crowd, phone screens visible, flash going off
- Light: Stage lights + phone flash + nothing; high contrast chaos
- Energy: Sweat, bodies, movement, noise visible in frame
- Emotional: Community, rebellion, underground, belonging
- Best For: Music brands, streetwear drops, event promotion, youth energy

**2. The Skate Fail**
- Setup: Skater attempting trick, failing, trying again
- Camera: Low angle, fisheye or wide, following movement
- Action: Real falls, real scrapes, real persistence
- Light: Outdoor sun or harsh park lights; no beauty treatment
- Sound: Wheels on concrete, board slapping, grunts, encouragement
- Emotional: Resilience, authenticity, process over perfection, culture
- Best For: Footwear, boards, streetwear, energy drinks, youth brands

**3. The Mirror Fit Check**
- Setup: Subject in bedroom/bathroom mirror showing outfit
- Camera: Phone selfie aesthetic; vertical format
- Environment: Real room — bed unmade, posters on wall, clothes on floor
- Action: Turning, adjusting, showing details to camera
- Graphics: Text overlay with fit details, brand tags, price, "WDYWT"
- Emotional: Self-expression, peer validation, identity construction
- Best For: Fashion, social content, community building, drop culture [^52^]

**4. The Night Crawl**
- Setup: Group moving through city at night — streets, trains, rooftops
- Camera: Handheld, phone, action cam; multiple perspectives
- Light: Neon, sodium vapor, phone screens, car headlights
- Pacing: Fast cuts on movement; music-driven rhythm
- Environment: Urban texture — graffiti, wet pavement, construction, bodegas
- Emotional: Freedom, danger, exploration, urban ownership
- Best For: Footwear, streetwear, beverages, tech, automotive youth

**5. The DIY Studio**
- Setup: Creator in bedroom/studio making something — screen printing, customizing, designing
- Camera: Close, raw, process-focused
- Environment: Cluttered creative space — paint cans, fabric, screens, laptops
- Light: Desk lamp, window, ring light; uneven and real
- Action: Hands working, mistakes happening, finished piece revealed
- Emotional: Creation, entrepreneurship, hustle, authenticity
- Best For: Creative tools, streetwear brands, art supplies, platforms

**6. The Drop Day Queue**
- Setup: Line outside store or digital countdown/unboxing
- Camera: Documentary style — real people, real anticipation
- Environment: Storefront, warehouse, or bedroom with delivery box
- Action: Waiting, unboxing, first look, reaction
- Graphics: "SOLD OUT", limited numbers, collaboration logos [^52^]
- Emotional: Hype, scarcity, community, cultural capital
- Best For: Product launches, collaborations, resale culture, brand heat
