# Food & Beverage Cinematography

## Core Philosophy

Food cinematography is the craft of making the viewer salivate through their eyes. The image must transmit temperature, texture, aroma, and sound without those senses being available. Every decision serves appetite appeal.

## Appetite Appeal Fundamentals

### The Triad of Desire
- **Color saturation**: Freshness reads as vibrant color. Dull, desaturated food reads as old, frozen, or unappetizing. Protect natural saturation.
- **Surface moisture**: The glisten of sauce, the dew on produce, the bead on a cold glass. Dry surfaces read as stale.
- **Texture differentiation**: Crispy vs. soft, creamy vs. chunky, smooth vs. seared. Contrast creates interest.

### Hero Angle Vocabulary
- **The 45-degree plate**: Slightly above, looking down at 30-45 degrees. Shows surface and edge. The standard menu angle.
- **The overhead (flat lay)**: 90 degrees above. Graphic, pattern-based, editorial. Strong for plates with arrangement and color.
- **The 10-degree rake**: Almost profile, barely above the plate. Shows height, stack, and architecture. The burger angle.
- **The macro ingredient**: Isolated element filling frame. The single raspberry, the coffee bean, the salt crystal.

## Lighting for Edibility

### The Edibility Rule
Light must make food look like it would taste good. Hard, specular light on protein can read as greasy or artificial. Flat light on bread can read as doughy.

### Key Approaches
- **Backlight for translucency**: Light from behind or behind-side for 3-8 o'clock position. Makes greens glow, shows liquid density, creates rim on glassware.
- **Side light for texture**: Raking light across the surface reveals texture and structure. Essential for bread, pastry, grilled surfaces.
- **Top soft for evenness**: Large overhead source for table scenes, overhead plating, and flat lays. Prevents harsh shadows that compete with composition.
- **Fill card discipline**: White or silver bounce to open shadows. Gold bounce for warmth on comfort foods. Never let shadow side go completely black unless the mood is specifically dark.

### Light Direction by Food Type

| Food Type | Primary Light | Rationale |
|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| Soups and stews | Backlight or underlight | Shows liquid body, color saturation, steam |
| Grilled / seared meat | Side or 3/4 back | Reveals char texture, edge crispness, juice sheen |
| Baked goods / bread | Side light at 45 | Shows crumb texture, crust variation, flake |
| Salad / greens | Strong backlight | Translucency of leaves, freshness, water content |
| Chocolate / dessert | Soft, controlled | Prevents bloom; shows gloss and temper |
| Fried food | Raking side light | Crisp edge, oil sheen, structural integrity |
| Ice cream / frozen | Soft, diffused | Prevents melt under lights; shows scoop texture |

## Steam, Smoke, and Condensation

### Steam Generation
- **Practical steam**: Fresh-cooked food emits real steam. Capture immediately; steam dies in 30-60 seconds.
- **Artificial steam**: Steamer wands, humidifiers, or cigarette-sized smoke pellets. Direct upward; it rises.
- **Backlight steam**: Steam visible only when backlit or side-lit against dark background. Front-lit steam disappears.
- **Speed**: Steam shot at 60-120fps extends the tendrils and creates ethereal movement.

### Condensation and Temperature
- **Cold glass**: Glycerin + water mixture sprayed evenly. Creates persistent, controllable beads.
- **Frost**: Actual freezing followed by brief exposure. Shave frost for pattern or keep even.
- **Melt**: Ice cream must show slight melt at edges for "real" reading. Too perfect is artificial.

## Macro Depth of Field

### Ingredient Isolation
- **100mm macro at f/2.8-f/4**: Shallow depth isolates a single element. The water droplet on a leaf, the salt grain on a pretzel.
- **Focus stacking**: For product packaging where full depth is required. Multiple frames at different focus planes combined.
- **Texture planes**: The rough cookie against the smooth chocolate against the crisp nut. Three depths, each sharp.

### Pour and Cascade
- **Liquid pour**: Syrup, cream, honey. Side backlight shows viscosity and column integrity. 60fps minimum for smoothness.
- **The perfect break**: The stream separating from the source. The droplet detaching. The splash crown forming. High-speed capture.
- **Glassware fill**: The rising meniscus, the bubble formation, the head on beer. Each has a specific visual signature.

## Beverage Specifics

### Beer and Carbonation
- **Head formation**: The foam must be fresh, not collapsed. Time the pour; shoot within 10 seconds.
- **Bubble streams**: Visible carbonation rising. Backlight or strong side light for bubble edge.
- **Glass treatment**: Impeccably clean. Any smudge or lipstick destroys the frame.

### Wine and Spirits
- **Color density**: The gradient from center to edge in the glass. Thin pour shows color; thick shows opacity.
- **Legs and tears**: The viscous rivulets on glass interior. Swirl and wait; shoot immediately as they form.
- **Ice and dilution**: Large format ice, hand-cut, slow-melting. The crack and fissure are character.

### Coffee and Tea
- **Crema**: The foam on espresso. Golden, persistent, tiger-striping in the caramel surface.
- **Steam from cup**: The classic "coffee warmth" cue. Backlight against dark background.
- **Pour over**: The spiral pour, the bloom, the extraction color change. The ritual as beauty.

## The Kitchen in Motion

### Process Cinematography
- **Hands and tools**: The knife work, the whisk, the flip. Skill transmission. Camera at counter height or slightly above.
- **Fire and heat**: The blue jet of gas, the orange flare of oil, the ember glow. Color temperature contrast.
- **Transformation**: Before and after in the same frame. The raw ingredient becoming cooked. The batter becoming cake.

### Chef as Subject
- **The chef's eye**: Close on the critical evaluation. The tilt of the head, the assessing gaze.
- **The plating gesture**: Tweezers, squeeze bottle, spoon back. Precision and confidence.
- **Environment**: The professional kitchen as stainless theater. Clean, active, purposeful.

## Composition and Styling

### The Plate as Stage
- **Odd numbers**: 3, 5, 7 elements. More dynamic than even pairing.
- **The bite mark**: The taken bite shows scale, suggests taste, creates invitation.
- **The garnish as punctuation**: Not decoration—meaning. The herb that names the cuisine, the color that balances the plate.

### Environment Context
- **The table setting**: Napkin fold, silver placement, glass position. The world around the plate.
- **The messy real**: Crumbs, sauce drip, used utensils. The evidence of enjoyment vs. the untouched ideal.
- **Cultural cues**: Chopsticks, flatbread, communal plate. The eating culture implied.

## Grade and Color

### Saturation Strategy
- **Produce first**: Vegetables and fruits receive maximum saturation protection. Meat second. Background last.
- **Warmth**: Food reads warmer than neutral. Slight yellow-red lift in midtones increases appetite.
- **The green trap**: Green vegetables shift yellow-green easily. Protect against overcooking via grade.

## Quick Reference: Food Frame Checklist

Before approving any food frame:

- [ ] Food looks fresh, never tired or wilted
- [ ] Surface moisture present where appropriate
- [ ] Steam / condensation visible if temperature story requires it
- [ ] Highlight not blown on glossy surfaces; detail preserved
- [ ] Color saturation natural to the ingredient; no channel clipping
- [ ] Hero element in sharpest plane; depth serves hierarchy
- [ ] Appetite appeal immediate; the viewer should feel hunger
- [ ] Background supports and separates; does not distract
- [ ] Props and environment culturally and contextually appropriate
- [ ] No unintended reflections, smudges, or imperfections on glass/flatware
