# Video Editing & Structure — General Purpose

## The Three-Act Structure (Universal)

### Act 1: Hook (0-15 seconds)
- **Goal**: Stop the scroll. Create immediate curiosity or tension.
- Techniques: Start with the payoff, not the intro. Show the most compelling moment first, then rewind.
- Never start with "Hey guys" or a logo animation.

### Act 2: Build (15 seconds - end)
- **Goal**: Deliver on the hook's promise while maintaining engagement.
- Techniques: Pattern interrupts every 3-7 seconds (cut, zoom, text overlay, B-roll change).
- Each segment should answer one question and raise another.

### Act 3: Resolution/CTA (last 10%)
- **Goal**: Leave the viewer with a clear takeaway or next action.
- Techniques: Summarize key points, pose a thought-provoking question, direct to specific next step.

## Pacing Principles

| Video Length | Ideal Cut Frequency | Best For |
|-------------|-------------------|----------|
| Under 60s (Shorts/Reels) | Every 1-3 seconds | Quick tips, visual showcases, hooks |
| 1-5 minutes | Every 3-7 seconds | Tutorials, reviews, explainers |
| 5-15 minutes | Every 5-15 seconds | Deep dives, documentaries |
| 15+ minutes | Every 10-30 seconds | Lectures, interviews, long-form |

## Visual Storytelling Techniques

### Show, Don't Tell
- Instead of "This benchmark is fast," show the benchmark running with a timer overlay
- Instead of "The design is clean," show before/after comparison

### Pattern Interrupts (keep attention)
- Cut to different angle
- Zoom in/out dramatically
- Text overlay appears/disappears
- Color grade shift
- Sound effect or music change
- B-roll insert

### Transitions That Work
- **Hard cut**: Most common, most effective. Don't overuse fancy transitions.
- **Match cut**: Connect two shots by similar visual element (shape, color, motion)
- **J-cut/L-cut**: Audio from next scene starts before/after video cuts — smooth and professional
- **Whip pan**: Quick camera movement that becomes the transition — use sparingly

## Audio Design Principles

### Layer 1: Voice/Narration
- Must be clear and intelligible above all else
- Remove breaths, ums, pauses (unless intentional for pacing)
- Normalize to -3dB peak

### Layer 2: Music
- Should support emotion, not compete with voice
- Duck music by 6-10dB when someone is speaking
- Change music at act transitions or major topic shifts

### Layer 3: Sound Effects
- Subtle whooshes on cuts/transitions
- UI sounds for screen recordings
- Ambient noise for atmosphere
- Less is more — if you notice the SFX, it's too much

## Editing Checklist

Before finalizing any video:
- [ ] First 5 seconds grab attention immediately
- [ ] No dead air or awkward pauses (unless intentional)
- [ ] Visual changes happen at least every 7 seconds
- [ ] Text overlays are readable (large enough, good contrast)
- [ ] Audio levels consistent throughout
- [ ] Music doesn't overpower narration
- [ ] Ending has clear takeaway or CTA
- [ ] No technical artifacts (glitches, audio pops, dropped frames)

## Common Editing Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Long intro/logo | Viewers leave before content starts | Start with content; put branding at end or as subtle watermark |
| Static shots for too long | Visual boredom kills retention | Add zoom, pan, cut to B-roll, overlay graphics |
| Music too loud | Voice becomes unintelligible | Duck music during speech; use -6dB minimum ducking |
| Overusing transitions | Distracts from content | Use hard cuts 90% of the time; reserve effects for emphasis |
| No clear ending | Viewer doesn't know what to do next | End with summary, question, or specific CTA |

## File Format Quick Reference

| Format | Best For | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| MP4 (H.264) | Universal compatibility, web upload | Safe default for everything |
| MP4 (H.265/HEVC) | High quality at smaller file size | Better compression but slower to encode |
| ProRes | Editing master files | Large files, preserves quality for further editing |
| WAV | Audio only, highest quality | Use for narration before mixing into video |
