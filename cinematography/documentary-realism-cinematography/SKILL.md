# Documentary Realism Cinematography

## Core Philosophy

Documentary cinematography is the ethics of looking. The camera must enter a real situation, capture what occurs, and exit without leaving damage. The image serves truth before beauty, though the two are not mutually exclusive. The presence of the camera must be acknowledged or fully absorbed; there is no middle ground that does not betray.

## Handheld Discipline

### The Grammar of Presence
- **The settled frame**: Handheld does not mean constantly moving. Hold on subject, allow micro-movement, breathe with the scene.
- **The purposeful shift**: Reposition when the action demands it, not to "keep it interesting." The camera moves for reason, not for style.
- **Shoulder mount stability**: The most natural human scale. Slight vertical float from breathing, horizontal drift from attention.
- **The two-hand grip / rig**: More stable than shoulder, less stable than tripod. The engaged observer.

### Breathing and Heartbeat
- **The body as shock absorber**: Knees slightly bent, weight balanced. The camera becomes part of the operator's mass.
- **The inhale / exhale**: Shoot on exhale for minimum body motion. The sniper's discipline applied to framing.
- **Walking shots**: The heel-to-toe roll, the counter-hip swing. Practice the gait that minimizes vertical bounce.

### When to Hold Still
- **The reaction shot**: When someone receives news, the camera holds. The face fills the frame. The operator disappears.
- **The confessional**: Interview, testimony, emotional release. Lock off or settle completely. The subject deserves stability.
- **The landscape of place**: Establishing shots, environment detail. The tripod or monopod as respect for the space.

## Available Light

### Working with What Exists
- **Assessment first**: Before adding any light, observe what is present. Note direction, quality, color temperature, and sources.
- **The motivation rule**: Any added light must be justifiable by an existing source. A lamp in frame motivates fill.
- **Practical enhancement**: Increase existing practical bulbs (higher wattage, daylight-balanced) rather than introducing alien sources.
- **Window management**: The documentary's key light. Diffuse with trace or net if too harsh. Flag to control spill. Never fight a window without cause.

### Exposure Strategy
- **Protect skin**: In mixed conditions, expose for the subject's face. Allow windows to blow if necessary. The person matters more than the view.
- **The silhouette choice**: When a subject is backlit and no fill is possible, commit to silhouette or partial silhouette. The half-lit face is a statement.
- **Noise acceptance**: In low light, a noisy image of a critical moment is preferable to no image or a lit image that destroys the moment. Modern noise reduction is sophisticated.

### Color Temperature in Documentary
- **Mixed light reality**: Offices with fluorescent overheads and tungsten desk lamps. Homes with daylight windows and warm bulbs. This is real; do not automatically "correct" it.
- **The white balance choice**: Daylight preset for consistency in run-and-gun. Custom white balance for controlled situations. The choice is editorial.
- **The neon / sodium / mercury world**: Streetlights, signs, industrial lighting. These colors locate the subject in a specific world. Preserve them.

## Unposed Naturalism

### The Invisible Camera
- **Time as tool**: The first hour, the camera is foreign. The second hour, tolerated. The third hour, forgotten. Budget time for absorption.
- **The wide shot refuge**: When subjects become self-conscious, widen out. The long lens from distance reduces performance instinct.
- **The cutaway library**: Hands, feet, environment, objects. These shots cover edits and release performance pressure from the subject.

### Direct Address and Observation
- **Observational (fly-on-the-wall)**: No direct address, no interview within scene. The camera watches. The audience forgets the mediator.
- **Interactive (participatory)**: The filmmaker is heard or seen. The relationship is part of the story. The camera is acknowledged.
- **Reflexive**: The filmmaking process itself is visible. The microphone, the question, the setup. The audience is never allowed to forget mediation.

### The Ethics of the Close-Up
- **Intimacy earned**: A tight frame implies relationship. Do not punch in on strangers without contextual justification.
- **The crying face**: The most powerful and most exploited image. Hold on it only if the subject has consented to this level of exposure. Cut away if the moment is too private.
- **The sleeping body**: Vulnerable, unguarded. Requires deep trust. The documentary equivalent of nudity.

## Interview and Testimony

### The Setup
- **The look**: Slightly off-camera, past the interviewer. The classic documentary address. Never direct to lens unless deliberate to-camera address.
- **Eye line height**: Camera at or slightly below eye level. Looking slightly up conveys authority; slightly down, reflection.
- **Background as context**: The subject's environment visible behind them. Bookshelf, workshop, street. The background must be informative, not distracting.

### Lighting the Interview
- **Key from the look side**: Light the side of face that is toward their look direction. Motivated by the window or a practical.
- **Soft source**: Large source, close to subject. The documentary interview is not about dramatic shadow; it is about clarity and presence.
- **Background light**: Separate background from subject. A dark hole behind a subject reads as isolation; a bright window reads as context.
- **The three-point refinement**: Key, fill, back/hair. But keep it gentle. The interview must not feel "produced."

### The Lens Choice
- **85-135mm**: The standard interview lens. Flattering compression, background separation, intimate scale.
- **50mm**: When space demands or environmental context is important. Slightly more distortion, more environment.
- **Avoid wide angles**: Unless the environment is the subject. Wide-angle interviews read as distortion and invasion.

## The Long Take

### Vérité and the Extended Frame
- **The scene in real time**: No cut, no coverage. The camera finds what matters within continuous reality.
- **The zoom in documentary**: Slow, imperceptible zooms to reframe. The zoom as attention shift, not style statement.
- **The pan and reveal**: Hold on one subject, pan to reveal another. The camera as discovery.

### Coverage without Interruption
- **The two-camera minimum**: Wide and tight, or two angles. Allows edit without stopping the scene.
- **The safety wide**: Always have a wide shot running. The parachute for the edit.
- **Sound as anchor**: In vérité, sound often carries the edit. The camera drifts; the sound sustains continuity.

## Place and Environment

### The Establishing Philosophy
- **The wide shot is not decoration**: It must contain information. The neighborhood, the weather, the time of day, the economic context.
- **The detail as evidence**: The worn step, the stack of papers, the empty plate. These tell the story without exposition.
- **The empty room**: After the subject has left, the space retains memory. The absence as presence.

### Working in Real Locations
- **Permission and respect**: Leave the space as found. Do not rearrange for camera without explicit consent.
- **The compromise**: Sometimes the truth is in the corner with bad light. Shoot it. Do not force the subject to the window for "better" light if it breaks the scene.
- **The cluttered real**: Real homes and offices are full. Do not strip them for aesthetic cleanliness unless that is the story.

## Posture and Ethics

### The Filmmaker's Presence
- **Transparency**: When possible, show the crew, the microphone, the process. The audience trusts what they understand.
- **Consent as ongoing**: A subject who agreed at 9 AM may feel differently at 9 PM after an emotional scene. Check in.
- **The cut as protection**: Some moments must not be shown. The cut is as ethical a decision as the shoot.

## Quick Reference: Documentary Shot Checklist

Before approving any documentary frame:

- [ ] The situation is real, not performed for camera (unless performance is the subject)
- [ ] The subject has consented to this level of exposure
- [ ] Camera movement motivated by scene energy, not operator restlessness
- [ ] Light appears motivated by existing sources
- [ ] Exposure prioritizes subject over environment where conflict exists
- [ ] Background informs; not distracting or stripped bare
- [ ] Sound context is present or achievable in the frame
- [ ] Intimacy level appropriate to relationship with subject
- [ ] The frame respects the dignity of the person and place
- [ ] The image serves understanding before beauty
