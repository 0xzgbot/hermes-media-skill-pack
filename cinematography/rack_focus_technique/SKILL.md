# SKILL: Rack Focus Technique
## Version: 1.0 | Hermes Agent Camera & Lens Language

---

### DESCRIPTION
Mastery of focal plane transitions within a continuous shot. Rack focus (also called "pulling focus") shifts sharpness from one subject to another, directing viewer attention, revealing narrative information, and connecting spatially separated elements. The technique functions as visual punctuation — a comma, question mark, or exclamation point within the shot [^20^][^27^].

### TRIGGER KEYWORDS
rack focus, pull focus, focus pull, shallow depth of field transition, focus shift, focal plane change, foreground to background focus, background reveal, focus change, bokeh to sharp, focus transition, depth of field pull

### CORE RULES
- Rack focus requires large aperture (shallow DOF): f/1.4–f/2.8 typically; f/5.6+ won't work
- Direction matters: foreground-to-background = reveal, background-to-foreground = return/arrival
- Speed of focus pull carries meaning: fast = surprise/urgency, slow = considered/revelatory
- Both subjects must be within the lens's focus range — plan spatial separation
- The subject losing focus should blur smoothly: lens choice affects bokeh quality during pull
- Best used for narrative reveals: new information entering frame, or returning to a character
- Avoid in fast-paced action — rack focus is a moment of contemplation or transition

---

### TECHNICAL PARAMETERS

**Mechanical Definition:**
- Action: Changing lens focus ring during continuous recording
- Result: Focal plane shifts from one distance to another
- Alternative Names: Pulling focus, focus pull, rack focusing [^27^]
- Professional Role: Focus Puller (1st AC) executes on set [^27^]

**Depth of Field Requirements:**
- Shallow DOF: f/2.8 or lower creates dramatic, noticeable shifts [^23^]
- Deep DOF: f/8+ creates subtle, almost subliminal transitions
- Noticeability: Shallower aperture = more dramatic blur-to-sharp transition
- Control Trade-off: Shallow DOF is harder to execute precisely but more cinematic [^23^]

**Distance Between Subjects:**
- Minimum Separation: Subjects must be at significantly different focal distances
- Dramatic Separation: 3+ feet between focal planes for clear effect
- Overlap Risk: If subjects too close, shift becomes subtle or unnoticeable [^23^]
- Measurement: Focus puller marks distances on lens/follow focus wheel [^22^]

**Speed & Timing:**
- Fast Rack: 0.5–1 second — sudden revelation, shock, action response
- Slow Rack: 2–4 seconds — emotional realization, contemplation, romance
- Narrative Sync: Must align with story beat (dialogue pause, reaction moment) [^20^]
- Rhythm: Like choreography — practice required for precision [^23^]

**Lens Characteristics:**
- Longer Lenses: 85mm+ exaggerate focus shift dramatically but are harder to keep sharp [^23^]
- Wide Lenses: 24–35mm make shift more subtle due to deeper DOF
- Focus Breathing: Lens magnification change during focus pull (some lenses exhibit this)
- Throw: Distance focus ring travels between near and far marks

### ADVANCED TECHNIQUES

**1. The Revelation Rack**
- Start: Foreground subject sharp (character, object)
- End: Background detail sharp (threat, clue, important element)
- Speed: Medium to slow (1–2 seconds)
- Emotional: "Look at this" — discovery, realization, dread
- Example: Character talking, rack focus reveals someone listening in background [^31^]

**2. Emotional Connection Rack**
- Setup: Two characters in frame at different distances
- Action: Focus shifts from Speaker to Listener
- Speed: Slow, gentle (2–3 seconds)
- Emotional: Relationship dynamics, unspoken reaction, empathy shift
- Example: The Social Network deposition scene — focus shifts between Zuckerberg and lawyers [^23^]

**3. Object-to-Face Rack**
- Start: Object in foreground sharp (letter, weapon, phone)
- End: Character's face sharp reacting to object
- Speed: Medium (1–2 seconds)
- Emotional: Comprehension, decision, emotional response
- Example: Character reads message, focus pulls to their devastated expression [^20^]

**4. Threat Reveal Rack (Horror/Suspense)**
- Start: Character in foreground, unaware
- End: Background threat coming into focus (monster, attacker)
- Speed: Slow build then snap (2 seconds building, 0.5 second snap)
- Emotional: Dread, suspense, helplessness
- Example: Character looks in mirror, focus shifts to reflection behind them

**5. Environmental Storytelling Rack**
- Start: Character sharp in foreground
- End: Background environment detail sharp (set decoration, location clue)
- Speed: Slow, contemplative (3–4 seconds)
- Emotional: Context, world-building, atmosphere
- Example: Character at bar, focus shifts to row of glasses revealing something [^29^]

**6. Internal Thought Rack**
- Start: Character's face sharp, reacting
- End: Distant memory/object of desire sharp
- Speed: Very slow, dreamlike (4+ seconds)
- Emotional: Memory, longing, psychological depth
- Visual: May include slight diffusion or color shift on memory end

| Speed | Duration | Emotional Effect | Narrative Use |
|-------|----------|------------------|---------------|

### TECHNICAL NOTES FOR AI GENERATION
- Use "rack focus" or "focus pull" explicitly to trigger the technique
- Specify which element starts sharp and which ends sharp
- Include "shallow depth of field" or "f/2.8" for pronounced blur transition
- Mention "smooth transition" to prevent abrupt focus jumps
- Specify speed/duration ("2-second pull", "slow rack") for emotional pacing
- Use "continuous shot" to emphasize no-cut technique
- Mention "bokeh" for the out-of-focus plane quality
