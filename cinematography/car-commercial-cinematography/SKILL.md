# Car Commercial Cinematography

## Core Philosophy

Automotive cinematography treats the vehicle as a living sculpture. Every surface, reflection, and motion decision serves to amplify perceived value, performance, and emotional desire. The craft balances technical precision with visceral impact.

## Hero Angle Vocabulary

### The Power Three-Quarter (3/4 Front)
- **Position**: Camera 15-30 degrees off the front centerline, lens at beltline height (typically 30-40 inches)
- **Purpose**: Communicates authority, stance, and front design language
- **Critical note**: The leading headlight must catch a specular highlight; shadow side must retain visible detail in grille and fascia sculpting
- **Wheel treatment**: Front wheel turned 8-15 degrees toward camera to expose rim depth

### The Profile (Pure Side)
- **Position**: True 90-degree perpendicular, camera at exact mid-height of the vehicle body
- **Purpose**: Reveals proportion, length, and silhouette character line
- **Critical note**: Never shoot at suspension settle; vehicle must be at ride height or 5-10mm below for athletic stance
- **Background rule**: Clean gradient or architectural element that echoes the vehicle's design era

### The Rear Three-Quarter (3/4 Back)
- **Position**: 20-35 degrees off the rear centerline, slightly elevated to read decklid and taillight graphic
- **Purpose**: Width, stability, and tail design communication
- **Critical note**: Taillights must read as shapes, not just red blobs; ensure exposure holds filament texture

### Low Frontal (The Predator)
- **Position**: 8-14 inches from ground plane, centered or slightly offset toward the light
- **Purpose**: Intimidation, scale, and road dominance
- **Critical note**: Ground surface must be considered part of the composition—wet asphalt, brushed concrete, or purposeful texture

### The Aerial (Dynamic Overhead)
- **Position**: Drone or crane at 20-40 feet, tracking the vehicle path
- **Purpose**: Context, scale, and environment integration
- **Critical note**: Shadow of the vehicle on the road surface is a compositional element; time it for maximum graphic impact

## Chrome and Paint Reflection Management

### Reflection Architecture
- **Primary light source**: Large soft source (12x12 or 20x20 frame) positioned to create a gradient reflection along the body line
- **Secondary accent**: Hard source for edge highlight (the "slash" that defines the shoulder line)
- **Sky management**: Overcast is preferred; direct sun requires overhead silk or polarizer with ND

### Paint Finishes by Type

| Finish Type | Key Behavior | Lighting Approach |
|-------------|-----------|-------------------|
| Solid/Metallic | Shows source shape directly; metallic flake creates texture under hard light | Large soft source with fill 2 stops below key |
| Pearl/Tricoat | Color shifts with angle; soft light preserves nuance | Wraparound soft; avoid hard cuts |
| Matte/Satin | Absorbs light; shows every surface imperfection | Flat even light; flag all direct specular |
| Black | Mirror-like at oblique angles; burns out under point sources | Giant soft source; no direct sky in reflection |
| White | Defines form through shadow only; easily goes flat | Strong side key with deep shadow ratio |

### Chrome and Brightwork
- Chrome is a mirror: it reflects the world, not just lights
- Compose reflections intentionally: dark shapes against bright, architectural elements, or controlled gradient
- Flag unwanted elements rigorously; every chrome reflection tells a story
- Badge and emblem lighting: small focused spots or fiber to make lettering pop

## Motion Blur and Road Dynamics

### Background Blur vs. Wheel Blur
- **Background motion blur**: Subject sharp, world streaks. Conveys speed. Requires camera tracking or rig
- **Wheel rotation blur**: Must be present in any moving hero shot. Frozen wheels kill the illusion instantly
- **Rule of thumb**: At 24fps, 1/100-1/200 shutter gives natural wheel blur; 1/50-1/80 for more aggressive streaking

### Rig Types and Language
- **Russian Arm / pursuit arm**: Dramatic tracking shots, sweeping arcs, intimate proximity
- **Process trailer**: Actor driving shots; background plates composited or live
- **Moto rig / suction mount**: Low, dynamic perspectives attached to vehicle body
- **Cable cam / high-speed winch**: Linear acceleration or deceleration paths

### Road Texture as Character
- **Wet asphalt**: Reflects vehicle, doubles graphic presence, creates noir mood
- **Fresh seal / tar**: Rich black, texture holds tire detail
- **Concrete / brushed aggregate**: Industrial, urban, modern context
- **Cobblestone / historic paver**: Rhythmic vibration in tracking shots, European luxury cue
- **Salt flats / dry lake**: Infinite reflection, isolation, top-speed context
- **Snow / ice**: Elemental challenge, traction drama, Scandinavian aesthetic

## Studio Protocol

### Cyc Wall and Floor
- Seamless transition between vertical and horizontal surfaces
- Floor must be cleanable; vehicles leak, drip, and mark
- Cyc color: white for maximum flexibility, medium gray for controlled contrast, black for dramatic isolation

### Turntable Work
- 360-degree continuous rotation for configurators and interactive
- Stop-motion intervals for specific angle packages (every 15 degrees)
- Lighting must work at all angles or be dynamically adjusted per position

### Rigging Safety
- Vehicle securing: four-point minimum on turntable
- Personnel exclusion zone during powered turntable operation
- Overhead lighting clearance for SUVs and trucks at maximum lift

## Lens and Camera Discipline

### Focal Length Conventions
- **24-35mm**: Environmental context, interior cabin, dramatic perspective distortion
- **50-85mm**: Naturalistic driving, human-scale relationship to vehicle
- **100-135mm**: Compressed hero angles, abstracted background, premium luxury feel
- **200mm+**: Isolated detail, traffic sequences, motorsport compression

### Anamorphic Considerations
- Oval bokeh in background highlights (streetlights, traffic, signage)
- Horizontal flare on light sources crossing the frame edge
- Slight cylindrical distortion on vehicle edges, often desirable for dynamism
- Focus falloff: eye goes to sharp badge, sharp headlight, sharp wheel

## Color and Finish Strategy

### Grade Direction by Segment
- **Luxury**: Deepened blacks, desaturated primaries, warm highlights, cool shadows, lifted blacks for creaminess
- **Performance**: Punched contrast, saturated corporate color in accent details, crushed blacks, sharp midtone contrast
- **Utility / Truck**: Earth tone preservation, naturalistic saturation, readable detail in all zones
- **EV / Future**: Clean whites, subtle teal shadows, minimal grain, clinical precision

## Quick Reference: Shot Checklist

Before approving any automotive frame:

- [ ] Body line read clearly on light and shadow side
- [ ] Wheels: position, rim detail, no frozen motion (if moving)
- [ ] Reflections: composed, intentional, no crew or equipment visible
- [ ] Glass: visible interior detail or purposeful opacity
- [ ] Ground: texture appropriate to segment and story
- [ ] Background: supports vehicle, does not compete
- [ ] Badges / emblems: readable or intentionally anonymous
- [ ] Headlights: catchlight present, not blown out
- [ ] Brake calipers: visible if performance story (consider red contrast)
- [ ] Tire sidewall: branding consistent with story era
